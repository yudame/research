**Prompt:**
```
Research sleep-dependent memory consolidation in young children, particularly ages 4-6, and the implications for optimizing daily learning schedules.

**Context:** Designing evidence-based daily schedules for Pre-K/Kindergarten programs. Target audience: educators and parents seeking to align instructional timing with neurodevelopmental principles.

**Specific questions to address:**
- How does sleep (naps and nighttime) affect memory consolidation in 4-6 year olds?
- What types of learning benefit most from sleep-dependent consolidation (declarative vs. procedural, motor skills, language)?
- Are there critical timing windows for instruction relative to sleep cycles?
- How does nap architecture (duration, timing, sleep stages) relate to learning outcomes?
- What individual differences exist (nappers vs. non-nappers, chronotypes)?
- How does sleep quality/quantity affect next-day learning capacity?

**Research methodology:**
- Prioritize peer-reviewed studies, meta-analyses, and authoritative sources
- Distinguish between correlation and causation in findings
- Report effect sizes and practical significance, not just statistical significance
- Note the study populations (ages, sample sizes, demographics) and whether findings generalize to 4-6 year olds specifically
- Compare individual studies against meta-analyses and systematic reviews
- Identify preliminary research vs. well-replicated findings
- Note funding sources and potential conflicts of interest when relevant
- Include contradictory findings and areas of scientific uncertainty
- Distinguish between laboratory studies and real-world classroom interventions
- Note measurement methods (polysomnography, actigraphy, behavioral tests)
- Cite specific studies, researchers, and sources throughout

**Output:** Comprehensive research report with extensive citations and source links. Focus on actionable insights for schedule design while maintaining scientific rigor.
```
