# Stablecoin Operational Infrastructure and Economics at Scale

## Introduction

Stablecoins have emerged as a pivotal component of the global financial system, transitioning from niche tools to essential infrastructure for payments, treasury, and liquidity strategies. This report delves into the operational infrastructure and economics of stablecoins at scale, focusing on industry analysis, case studies, and comparative analysis. The insights are drawn from a variety of sources, including industry reports, market research, and operational disclosures.

## Industry Analysis

### Operational Costs of Running a Compliant Stablecoin

Operating a stablecoin at scale involves significant costs across various domains, including headcount, infrastructure, compliance systems, custody fees, and audit costs. These costs vary depending on the supply levels, ranging from $100 million to over $100 billion.

1. **Headcount and Team Composition**: Major stablecoin issuers like Tether and Circle require a diverse team comprising compliance, legal, engineering, operations, and security roles. The headcount can vary significantly, but a large-scale operation might involve hundreds of employees. Compliance and legal teams are crucial due to the stringent regulatory requirements ([Merkle Science](https://www.merklescience.com/blog/genius-act-passes-what-2025s-stablecoin-bill-means-for-issuers-banks)).

2. **Infrastructure and Compliance Systems**: The infrastructure includes blockchain nodes, data centers, and software systems for transaction processing and monitoring. Compliance systems are essential to meet regulatory requirements, including Anti-Money Laundering (AML) and Know Your Customer (KYC) protocols ([ChainUp](https://www.chainup.com/blog/2025-crypto-regulations-recap/)).

3. **Custody Fees and Audit Costs**: Custody fees are incurred for securely storing the reserves backing the stablecoins, often in short-term U.S. Treasury bills and other secure financial instruments. Audit costs are significant, especially for issuers with large supply levels, as they require regular audits to ensure reserve backing and compliance ([Messari](https://messari.io/copilot/share/circle-and-tether-revenue-streams-cadc656f-b247-4baa-8e13-d68804801531?lid=8kasir1eqbh7)).

### Sustainable Business Models

Different stablecoin issuers have adopted various business models to ensure sustainability:

1. **Tether's Reserve Yield Model**: Tether generates revenue primarily through the yield on its reserves, which are invested in short-term U.S. Treasury bills and other financial instruments. This model has proven highly profitable, with Tether leading the stablecoin market in revenue ([Messari](https://messari.io/copilot/share/circle-and-tether-revenue-streams-cadc656f-b247-4baa-8e13-d68804801531?lid=8kasir1eqbh7)).

2. **Circle's Institutional Focus**: Circle focuses on institutional clients, offering services such as treasury management and liquidity solutions. This approach is complemented by their compliance and regulatory focus, ensuring trust and reliability in their operations ([Fireblocks](https://www.fireblocks.com/report/state-of-stablecoins)).

3. **Ethena's Yield-Bearing Approach**: Ethena offers yield-bearing stablecoins, providing users with interest on their holdings. This model attracts users looking for stable value with added financial benefits ([RebelFi](https://rebelfi.io/blog/the-rise-of-the-stablecoin-operations-stack-custody-compliance-yield-and)).

### Team Composition

The team composition for major stablecoin issuers typically includes:

- **Compliance and Legal**: Responsible for ensuring adherence to regulatory requirements and managing legal risks.
- **Engineering**: Focuses on the development and maintenance of blockchain infrastructure and software systems.
- **Operations**: Manages day-to-day activities, including transaction processing and customer support.
- **Security**: Ensures the protection of digital assets and infrastructure against cyber threats.

## Case Studies & Implementation

### Circle's Response to the SVB Crisis

During the Silicon Valley Bank (SVB) crisis, Circle faced significant challenges due to its exposure to the bank. Circle held $3.3 billion of its USDC reserves at SVB, which led to a depeg of USDC against the U.S. dollar. Circle's operational response involved:

- **Public Reassurances**: Circle publicly assured stakeholders that it would cover any shortfall using corporate resources and external capital if necessary ([Finance Magnates](https://www.financemagnates.com/cryptocurrency/svb-crisis-circle-escapes-usdc-depeg-with-regulatory-assurance/)).

- **Engagement with Regulators**: Circle worked closely with U.S. federal regulators to ensure the protection of SVB depositors, which helped stabilize the situation ([Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/in-the-shadow-of-bank-run-lessons-from-the-silicon-valley-bank-failure-and-its-impact-on-stablecoins-20251217.html)).

### Operational Failures: Terra/UST, Celsius, and FTX

The collapse of Terra/UST, Celsius, and FTX highlighted significant operational failures:

- **Terra/UST**: The algorithmic stablecoin model of Terra/UST failed due to a lack of adequate reserves and market confidence, leading to a death spiral as the peg broke.

- **Celsius**: The lending platform faced liquidity issues due to risky investment strategies and poor risk management, resulting in insolvency.

- **FTX**: The exchange's collapse was attributed to mismanagement of customer funds and lack of transparency, causing a loss of trust and liquidity crisis.

### Multi-Chain Stablecoin Operations

Operating stablecoins across multiple blockchain networks involves:

- **Deploying to Multiple Chains**: Stablecoins are deployed on various blockchain networks to enhance accessibility and liquidity.

- **Managing Bridges**: Cross-chain bridges are used to facilitate the transfer of stablecoins between different networks, ensuring interoperability.

- **Coordinating Upgrades**: Regular upgrades are necessary to maintain compatibility with network changes and improve security.

## Comparative Analysis

### Operational Maturity Levels

Established issuers like Tether and Circle have achieved high operational maturity levels, characterized by robust infrastructure, compliance systems, and market trust. In contrast, newer entrants like PayPal and bank-issued tokens are still developing their operational capabilities and market presence ([FinTech Weekly](https://www.fintechweekly.com/magazine/articles/stablecoins-2025-regulation-banks-fintech-digital-money-infrastructure)).

### Infrastructure Vendors

Stablecoin operations are powered by various infrastructure vendors, including:

- **Monitoring Platforms**: Tools for real-time transaction monitoring and risk assessment.
- **Compliance Tools**: Software solutions for AML and KYC compliance.
- **Custody Providers**: Secure storage solutions for digital assets and reserves ([CM Alliance](https://www.cm-alliance.com/cybersecurity-blog/top-10-stablecoin-development-companies-in-2025)).

## Conclusion

The stablecoin industry has evolved significantly, with stablecoins becoming integral to the global financial infrastructure. Operational costs, sustainable business models, and team composition are critical factors in running a compliant stablecoin at scale. Case studies of Circle's response to the SVB crisis and the failures of Terra/UST, Celsius, and FTX provide valuable insights into operational challenges and strategies. Comparative analysis highlights the differences in operational maturity between established and newer issuers, while infrastructure vendors play a crucial role in powering stablecoin operations.
