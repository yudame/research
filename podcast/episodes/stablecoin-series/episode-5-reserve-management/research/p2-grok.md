### Stablecoin Reserve Management and Custody Infrastructure: A Comprehensive 2025 Review

Stablecoins have evolved from niche digital assets to critical components of global finance, with their reserve management and custody infrastructure facing intensified scrutiny amid regulatory advancements. As of December 2025, the stablecoin market capitalization exceeds $200 billion, driven by institutional adoption and cross-border utility. This report examines recent developments in the last 12 months, practitioner perspectives, and regional/market insights, drawing from news sources, X discussions, and expert analyses. Emphasis is placed on transparency, compliance, and innovation in reserve backing—typically 1:1 with fiat or equivalents—while highlighting challenges like depeg risks and costs.

#### Recent Developments (December 2024–December 2025)
The past year marked a regulatory renaissance for stablecoins, shifting from ambiguity to structured frameworks. The U.S. GENIUS Act, enacted July 2025, establishes federal oversight for payment stablecoins, restricting issuance to permitted entities with 100% reserves in cash or short-term Treasuries, annual submissions, and anti-money laundering (AML) compliance. Issuers like Tether responded with USAT, a compliant stablecoin issued via Anchorage Digital, emphasizing redemption rights and transparency. Early compliance announcements include Ripple's RLUSD, which integrates BNY Mellon custody and Deloitte attestations for reserve verification. FDIC proposals further outline application processes for bank subsidiaries, aiming to expand regulated issuance while studying algorithmic variants.

The AICPA's 2025 Criteria, released March 2025, provide benchmarks for stablecoin reporting on asset-backed fiat-pegged tokens, fostering consistent disclosures. Adoption has accelerated, with entities like KPMG issuing attestations for issuers to demonstrate reserve integrity. By June 2025, proposals for controls supporting operations were opened for comment, enhancing trust in attestations. This has influenced major stablecoins, with PYUSD and RLUSD incorporating monthly reports aligned with these criteria.

MiCA enforcement in the EU, fully effective December 2024, mandates licensing, 1:1 reserves, and transparency for stablecoins (ARTs/EMTs). Progress includes delistings of non-compliant tokens and a shift to unified regimes, though challenges persist in enforcement gaps. One year in, MiCA has reshaped markets, with winners like Circle and Coinbase gaining from compliance. X discussions highlight its role in reducing "regulation by enforcement."

Real-time PoR announcements surged, with integrations like Chainlink for RLUSD enabling verifiable reserves. Fireblocks and SolsticeFi adopted similar tech for Solana-native stablecoins, emphasizing delta-neutral yields and transparency. Codex's on-chain PoR addresses trust issues, with real-time attestations via zero-knowledge proofs.

#### Practitioner Perspectives
Custody providers are optimistic about regulations. Fireblocks views GENIUS and MiCA as enabling stablecoin payments, with 86% readiness for scale. Anchorage positions itself for "on-shoring" via regulated issuance, snagging deals like RLUSD. BitGo stresses GENIUS's role in secure handling, with X discussions noting policy advancements.

Auditors emphasize controls: Deloitte's work on RLUSD/PYUSD focuses on asset-backed criteria for solvency. KPMG approaches attestations with AICPA alignment, issuing reports for transparency. Smaller CPA firms like Forvis Mazars highlight key considerations for compliance, including standardized reporting.

Industry sentiment on costs is mixed but positive: EY surveys show cost savings drive adoption, despite rising licensing expenses. Competitive impacts include market cap declines for non-compliant tokens, but regulated ones like RLUSD thrive. X threads reflect breakthrough sentiment, with compliance as a "green light."

#### Regional/Market Insights
Asian approaches vary: Singapore requires 100% reserves with MAS oversight, attracting RWA focus. Hong Kong's ordinance mandates HK$25M capital, liquid assets, and AML, with licenses expected early 2026. Japan emphasizes licensed yen-pegged stablecoins like JPYC. X posts note Hong Kong's conservative stance for stability.

RLUSD and PYUSD prioritize transparency: RLUSD ($1.26B cap) uses BNY Mellon custody, Deloitte attestations, and multichain expansion. PYUSD maintains 100% reserves with Paxos oversight, integrating mainstream platforms. No major crises, but DeFi depegs (e.g., three in Nov 2025) and wrapped asset instability eroded confidence. Stablecoins showed resilience, with runs mitigated by reserves.
