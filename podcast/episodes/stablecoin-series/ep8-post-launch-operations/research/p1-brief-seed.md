# Episode 8: Post-Launch Operations & Continuous Compliance

## Core Research Question

What does it take to operate a stablecoin at scale, and how do projects maintain stability, security, and regulatory compliance over time?

## Research Objectives

Investigate the operational realities of running a stablecoin after launch, from ongoing monitoring and compliance to governance decisions and incident response. Analyze how operational maturity separates successful long-term projects from those that fail or fade into irrelevance.

## Key Investigation Areas

### 1. Continuous Monitoring & Transparency Reporting
- What operational metrics must be monitored in real-time (peg stability, reserve balances, transaction volume, redemption queues)?
- How do projects maintain transparency (public dashboards, regular attestations, reserve updates)?
- What automated systems detect anomalies or potential issues?
- How frequently should reserve attestations be published (monthly under GENIUS Act, more often voluntarily)?
- What does it cost to maintain continuous compliance and reporting infrastructure?

### 2. Regulatory Compliance & Evolving Requirements
- How do projects track and adapt to changing regulations across jurisdictions?
- What ongoing reporting is required to regulators (reserve reports, transaction monitoring, suspicious activity reports)?
- How do compliance costs scale with stablecoin supply and transaction volume?
- What happens when new regulations require operational changes (example: MiCA's 2024 implementation)?
- How do projects maintain compliance when operating across multiple jurisdictions with conflicting rules?

### 3. Governance & Protocol Evolution
- What governance decisions arise post-launch (protocol upgrades, fee changes, collateral type additions, emergency interventions)?
- How centralized vs. decentralized are governance processes in practice (token votes, multi-sig committees, issuer control)?
- What does historical data show about governance participation rates?
- How do projects balance agility (responding to threats) with decentralization (community control)?
- What governance attacks or controversies have occurred, and how were they resolved?

### 4. Incident Response & Crisis Management
- What types of incidents require response (de-pegging events, smart contract bugs, custodian issues, regulatory actions)?
- What does incident response look like in practice (communication, technical fixes, reserve adjustments, trading halts)?
- How have major stablecoins responded to crises (Circle during SVB collapse, Tether during bank runs)?
- What playbooks and preparation exist before incidents occur?
- How quickly can emergency actions be taken (pause contracts, freeze assets, upgrade protocols)?

### 5. De-Pegging Scenarios & Recovery
- Under what conditions do stablecoins lose their peg (liquidity crises, reserve issues, loss of confidence)?
- What does empirical data show about recovery from de-pegging events?
- How do different stabilization mechanisms perform during de-pegging (arbitrage, market operations, interest rates)?
- What communication strategies help vs. hurt during peg stress?
- When does a temporary de-peg become permanent (Terra/UST)?

### 6. Scaling Operations & Infrastructure
- How do operational costs scale with stablecoin growth (custody fees, compliance staff, infrastructure, audits)?
- What infrastructure bottlenecks emerge at scale (redemption processing, customer support, monitoring systems)?
- How do projects manage increasing transaction volumes across multiple chains?
- What staffing is required (compliance, legal, engineering, operations, customer support)?
- At what scale do economies of scale vs. complexity costs dominate?

### 7. Ecosystem Development & Long-Term Sustainability
- How do projects support ongoing ecosystem development (developer grants, protocol integrations, market making)?
- What revenue models sustain operations long-term (transaction fees, reserve yields, services)?
- How do projects balance profit extraction with ecosystem reinvestment?
- What role does community development play in long-term success?
- What does sustainability look like without subsidies or speculative growth?

### 8. Security Operations & Threat Evolution
- What ongoing security practices are necessary (continuous audits, bug bounties, monitoring, threat intelligence)?
- How do threat actors evolve their attacks over time?
- What does it cost to maintain security operations at scale?
- How do projects respond to disclosed vulnerabilities (bug reports, zero-days)?
- What role do insurance and reserves play in mitigating security incidents?

## Research Methodology

- **Analyze operational incidents**: Study how major stablecoins have handled crises and operational challenges
- **Compare operational maturity**: Examine differences between established projects (USDT, USDC, DAI) and newer entrants
- **Evaluate transparency practices**: Review actual public reporting from different stablecoins
- **Study governance decisions**: Analyze controversial or significant governance actions and their outcomes
- **Estimate operational costs**: Calculate staffing, infrastructure, compliance, and custody expenses at different scales
- **Track regulatory evolution**: Document how regulations have changed and how projects adapted
- **Note conflicts of interest**: When operational decisions benefit issuers vs. users

## Key Questions to Answer

1. What operational infrastructure is required to run a compliant stablecoin at scale?
2. How much do ongoing compliance, custody, and operational costs total?
3. How do projects maintain peg stability during adverse market conditions?
4. What governance structures have proven most effective for protocol evolution?
5. How quickly can projects respond to security incidents or regulatory changes?
6. What separates stablecoins that maintain trust over time from those that erode it?
7. At what scale does a stablecoin become operationally sustainable?
8. How do projects balance transparency with operational security and competitive positioning?

## Critical Success Factors to Evaluate

- **Peg stability over time**: Consistency of $1.00 peg across market conditions
- **Transparency consistency**: Regularity and quality of public reporting
- **Incident response quality**: Speed and effectiveness of crisis management
- **Regulatory compliance**: Track record of meeting evolving requirements
- **Governance effectiveness**: Ability to make necessary changes without controversy
- **Operational resilience**: Uptime, reliability, and ability to handle volume spikes
- **Long-term sustainability**: Revenue sufficiency without subsidies or growth

## Sources to Prioritize

- Incident post-mortems from major stablecoins (SVB crisis, bank runs, de-pegging events)
- Governance proposal archives and voting records
- Reserve attestation reports over time (tracking changes in composition and transparency)
- Regulatory compliance filings and disclosures
- Security incident reports and bug bounty programs
- Operational cost disclosures (if available) or estimates from industry participants
- Academic research on stablecoin governance and operational risks
- Regulatory guidance on ongoing compliance requirements (GENIUS Act, MiCA implementation)
- Industry benchmarks for operational costs and staffing

## Approach to Uncertainty

- Acknowledge that operational details are often proprietary and not publicly disclosed
- Distinguish between how projects claim to operate and evidence of actual practices
- Note that incident response quality is only tested during actual incidents
- Recognize that regulatory requirements continue to evolve unpredictably
- Identify where operational challenges may not be apparent until reaching significant scale
- Report when governance appears centralized despite decentralization claims
- Consider that operational sustainability may depend on market conditions (bull vs. bear markets)

## Output Goals

The research should provide evidence-based insights into:
- What operational infrastructure and practices are necessary to run a stablecoin reliably
- How projects have responded to actual crises and what determines successful vs. failed responses
- What the true costs of ongoing compliance and operations are at different scales
- How governance structures affect a project's ability to evolve and respond to challenges
- What monitoring and transparency practices build vs. erode user trust
- Where operational risks and failure modes exist even for well-designed stablecoins
- What long-term sustainability looks like beyond initial launch excitement
- How operational maturity correlates with longevity and market position
- What realistic expectations should exist for post-launch resource requirements
