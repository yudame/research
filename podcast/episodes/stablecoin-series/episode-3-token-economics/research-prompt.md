# Episode 3: Token Economic Design & Stabilization Mechanisms

## Core Research Question

How do stablecoins maintain price stability, and what economic design choices determine their long-term sustainability?

## Research Objectives

Investigate the economic architecture underlying stablecoins, comparing different stabilization mechanisms, collateralization models, and monetary policy approaches. Analyze real-world performance data to understand which designs have proven resilient and which have failed.

## Key Investigation Areas

### 1. Collateralization Models
- What are the fundamental differences between fiat-backed, crypto-collateralized, algorithmic, and hybrid models?
- How do over-collateralization ratios affect stability vs. capital efficiency?
- What does empirical data show about peg stability across different collateral types during market stress?
- How do reserve compositions (cash, treasuries, commercial paper, crypto assets) impact risk profiles?

### 2. Stabilization Mechanisms
- What specific mechanisms do stablecoins use to maintain their peg (arbitrage, algorithmic minting/burning, interest rate adjustments, central market making)?
- Under what market conditions do these mechanisms succeed vs. fail?
- What does the Terra/UST collapse reveal about algorithmic stabilization limits?
- How do dual-token systems (e.g., Terra/LUNA, Frax/FXS) function, and what are their vulnerabilities?

### 3. Monetary Policy & Governance
- How do stablecoins implement monetary policy (supply expansion/contraction, interest rates, open market operations)?
- What governance structures exist for protocol parameters, collateral composition, and emergency interventions?
- Who holds governance rights in different models, and how concentrated is decision-making power?
- What conflicts of interest arise between token holders, users, and issuers?

### 4. Economic Sustainability vs. Subsidy Models
- How have subsidized yields (e.g., Anchor Protocol's 20% APY) driven adoption but created unsustainable dynamics?
- What is the difference between genuine economic value creation and Ponzi-like structures?
- How do transaction fees, seigniorage, and other revenue sources contribute to long-term viability?
- What does the data show about stablecoin adoption after subsidies end?

### 5. Stress Testing & Failure Analysis
- What market conditions have caused historical de-pegging events (liquidity crises, bank runs, collateral crashes)?
- How have different stablecoins responded to the same stress events (e.g., March 2020 "Black Thursday", May 2022 Terra collapse, March 2023 banking crisis)?
- What early warning indicators predict de-pegging risk?
- How do redemption mechanisms perform under extreme demand?

## Research Methodology

- **Prioritize empirical evidence**: Focus on actual market performance data, historical de-pegging events, and quantitative analysis of peg stability
- **Compare across models**: Analyze how USDT, USDC, DAI, UST, FRAX, and other major stablecoins have performed under similar conditions
- **Examine failure cases**: Study Terra/UST, Iron Finance, and other collapsed stablecoins to understand failure modes
- **Distinguish design from execution**: Separate theoretical economic design from implementation quality and governance decisions
- **Note study populations**: Consider whether findings from one market environment (e.g., DeFi bull market) generalize to other conditions
- **Report effect sizes**: When discussing peg deviations, provide specific data (e.g., "dropped to $0.88" vs. "lost peg")
- **Identify conflicts of interest**: Note when analysis comes from project teams, investors, or competitors

## Key Questions to Answer

1. What collateralization model provides the best balance of stability, capital efficiency, and user trust?
2. Can algorithmic stablecoins achieve long-term stability without external collateral?
3. How much over-collateralization is necessary for crypto-backed stablecoins during market crashes?
4. What governance structures prevent value extraction while enabling necessary protocol changes?
5. What revenue models can sustain stablecoin operations without relying on unsustainable subsidies?
6. How do different reserve compositions affect credit risk, liquidity risk, and regulatory treatment?

## Critical Success Factors to Evaluate

- **Peg stability metric**: Standard deviation from $1.00 across different market conditions
- **Redemption reliability**: Ability to maintain 1:1 redemptions during stress
- **Transparency**: Quality and frequency of reserve attestations
- **Capital efficiency**: Collateral ratio required vs. actual usage
- **Resilience**: Recovery time after de-pegging events
- **Regulatory compliance**: Ability to meet emerging reserve requirements

## Sources to Prioritize

- Academic papers on stablecoin stability mechanisms and failure analysis
- Regulatory frameworks (GENIUS Act reserve requirements, MiCA collateral rules)
- Historical market data from de-pegging events and stress periods
- Reserve attestation reports from major stablecoins
- Post-mortem analyses of failed stablecoins (Terra, Iron Finance, etc.)
- Central bank research on stablecoin risks (BIS, FSB, ECB, Federal Reserve)
- Empirical studies comparing peg stability across different models

## Approach to Uncertainty

- Clearly distinguish between proven mechanisms (fiat-backed with full reserves) and experimental approaches (algorithmic, fractional)
- Note where theoretical models have not been stress-tested at scale
- Identify findings that may not generalize from bull markets to bear markets
- Report contradictory evidence (e.g., DAI's successful recovery vs. UST's death spiral)
- Acknowledge that regulatory changes may retrospectively validate or invalidate certain models

## Output Goals

The research should provide evidence-based insights into:
- Which economic designs have demonstrated resilience across market cycles
- What trade-offs exist between stability, capital efficiency, decentralization, and regulatory compliance
- Where innovation has succeeded vs. where it has introduced unacceptable risks
- What lessons from historical failures should inform future designs
