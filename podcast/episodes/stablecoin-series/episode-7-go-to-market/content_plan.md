## NotebookLM Guidance

### Opening Instructions
Open with the dramatic contrast between Meta's Libra failure and 2025's stablecoin partnerships. In January 2022, Meta sold Libra/Diem for $182 million—the company that connected 3 billion people couldn't launch a currency. David Marcus called it "100% a political kill." Three years later, Visa settled stablecoin transactions on Solana, Stripe acquired Bridge for $1.1 billion, and Mastercard enabled stablecoin payouts. Use this contrast to establish the episode's thesis: The stablecoin GTM question has shifted from "who can mint the most tokens" to "who controls distribution and compliance."

### Key Terms to Define
| Term | Definition | Pronunciation |
|------|------------|---------------|
| Go-to-Market Strategy | The plan for launching and distributing a product to customers | |
| Network Effects | The phenomenon where a product becomes more valuable as more people use it | |
| Two-Sided Market | A platform that connects two distinct user groups (buyers/sellers, senders/receivers) | |
| Cold-Start Problem | The chicken-and-egg dilemma where you need users to have value but need value to attract users | |
| Distribution Rail | An existing payment network or platform used to deliver new products to users | |
| Regulatory Moat | A competitive advantage created by compliance with regulations that competitors cannot easily match | |
| Intention-Action Gap | The difference between what consumers say they'll do and what they actually do | |
| Protocol-Owned Liquidity | DeFi reserves owned by the protocol itself rather than rented from yield farmers | |
| Mercenary Capital | Investment that flows to the highest yield without loyalty, leaving when incentives end | |
| Edge Conversion | Converting stablecoins to fiat at the point of purchase, not requiring merchant crypto adoption | |

### Studies/Data to Emphasize
1. **FIS Consumer Survey (November 2025, n=1,000)** - 75% would try stablecoins via bank vs. 3.6% from unregulated providers
   - Why it matters: Bank trust is the binding constraint for consumer adoption

2. **CEX.io Q3 2025 Report / Visa-Allium Labs** - 71-90% of stablecoin volume is bot-driven
   - Why it matters: Headline volumes dramatically overstate genuine adoption

3. **Federal Reserve SHED Survey** - Crypto payment usage declined from ~3% to <2% (2021-2024)
   - Why it matters: Reveals major intention-action gap despite survey enthusiasm

4. **Libra/Diem Sale (Business Insider, Dec 2024)** - Sold for $182M despite Meta's 3B user distribution
   - Why it matters: Proves regulatory legitimacy is gating constraint, not add-on

5. **Formo DeFi Retention Study (2025)** - 7% six-month retention for incentive-acquired vs. 12.8% organic
   - Why it matters: Incentive-driven adoption is systematically less sustainable

6. **World Bank M-Pesa Case Study** - 40% of Kenyan adults in 3 years, 10% of GDP in monthly transfers
   - Why it matters: Provides actionable precedent for payment network adoption

### Narrative Arc

**Section 1: Foundation**
- Primary focus: The Distribution Problem—why having the best stablecoin doesn't guarantee anyone will use it
- Key analogy: "Think of launching a stablecoin like launching a new currency in a country where everyone already uses dollars. You can print beautiful bills, but if no one accepts them, they're just paper."
- Concepts to establish:
  1. The chicken-and-egg problem (need merchants to attract consumers, need consumers to attract merchants)
  2. Bot volume reality (71% automated) vs. genuine adoption (47M monthly active users)
  3. The consumer trust barrier (75% bank preference, 3.6% unregulated comfort)
- Transition hook: "So if traditional crypto approaches don't work for mainstream adoption, what does? The answer comes from an unexpected source: Kenya in 2007."

**Section 2: Evidence**
- Evidence cluster A: Where Stablecoins Actually Work
  - Remittance corridors: Mexico $63.3B, Nigeria $22B annually
  - Inflation hedging: Argentina 60% stablecoin activity
  - Institutional settlement: 90% of FIs now actively building (Fireblocks)
  - Geographic pattern: Emerging markets lead adoption out of necessity

- Evidence cluster B: Network Effects & Competition
  - USDT's 60% share through trading liquidity lock-in
  - USDC's regulatory strategy and MiCA advantage
  - Market fragmentation (88% to 82% combined share)
  - USDe's $13B growth via yield differentiation

- Evidence cluster C: Distribution Partnership Strategy
  - Visa + Bridge: 150M merchants, edge conversion at purchase
  - Stripe acquisition of Bridge for $1.1B, stablecoin financial accounts
  - Mastercard + Thunes: stablecoin payouts to wallets
  - PayPal PYUSD: 20M merchant target, closed-loop approach

- Evidence cluster D: Failed Strategies
  - Libra/Diem: "100% a political kill" despite world-class distribution
  - Terra/UST: 98.4% collapse in 7 days from unsustainable yields
  - Post-incentive TVL drops: Unichain -86%, Linea -83%, Berachain -91%

- Conflict to address: Why 75% say they'd try stablecoins via bank but <2% actually use crypto for payments
- Callback opportunity: "Remember Libra's failure with 3 billion potential users? That's what happens when you ignore the regulatory gating constraint."

**Section 3: Application**
- Protocol 1: Evaluating GTM Strategy (for projects/investors)
  - Distribution leverage: What existing network does this use?
  - Regulatory positioning: Can it pass GENIUS Act/MiCA requirements?
  - Merchant acceptance: How does fiat conversion happen at the edge?
  - Sustainability: Is growth driven by utility or incentives?

- Protocol 2: Historical Precedent Checklist (M-Pesa, Alipay, Zelle lessons)
  - Subsidize one side to bootstrap the network
  - Leverage existing platforms (banks, exchanges, fintechs)
  - Identify killer use case with high incumbent fees
  - Build infrastructure for others (Wise Platform model)
  - Solve trust through transparency (Alipay escrow model)

- Regulatory Context:
  - GENIUS Act (July 2025): Effective January 2027, federal stablecoin framework
  - MiCA: 60% EU bank deposit requirement forced Tether out
  - Bank entry: Bank of America, JPMorgan positioning for stablecoin issuance
  - Wyoming FRNT: First US state-issued stablecoin (August 2025)

- Caveats:
  - Consumer education gap: 42% incorrectly believe stablecoins are volatile
  - Generational divide: Gen Z 71% interested vs. Baby Boomers 18%
  - Retention remains challenging even with UX improvements
  - Enterprise case studies still limited in public disclosure

### Closing Instructions
- Callback to opening: "We started with Libra's $182 million failure despite connecting 3 billion people. Now you understand why: regulatory legitimacy isn't an optimization to pursue later—it's the gating constraint that determines whether you can play at all."
- Key takeaway: "The stablecoin GTM question has fundamentally shifted. Success no longer goes to whoever mints the most tokens. It goes to whoever controls distribution rails and regulatory compliance. The path to adoption runs through traditional finance, not around it."
- Sign-off: "Find the full research and sources at research dot yuda dot me—that's Y-U-D-A dot M-E."
