# Lightning Talk: The Science of Letting Go
## 10-Minute Outline — Yudame Research Ep. 8

**Audience:** Business owners, managers, team leads
**Tone:** Conversational but evidence-backed.

---

### 1. THE UNCOMFORTABLE TRUTH 

**Open with the study, then pull the rug on frameworks.**

- 20,000+ hires, 312 orgs, 3 years
  "Why New Hires Fail" (2005) - a study by Forbes writer Mark Murphy, published in Leadership IQ
- 82% of managers saw warning signs — hired anyway; 46% failed within 18 months
- 89% attitudinal (coachability, EQ, motivation, temperament) — only 11% technical
- Not "Can they do it?" but "Will they adapt when it breaks?"
- The frameworks we use? 70% Rule, RACI, SLII — none tested in a controlled study
- Thinking aids, not science

---

### 2. THE HIDDEN PREDICTOR - It's not IQ.

- Learning agility correlates with success at 0.74 — stronger than height predicts weight
- Uncorrelated with IQ (r = 0.09 across 60,000+ people). Smartest person in the room may be the worst delegate
- **Technique:** Give real feedback mid-interview. Curiosity = green. Defensiveness = red.

---

### 3. THE CULTURAL REVERSAL - More autonomy can backfire.

- Empowerment negatively associated with satisfaction in India
- Norwegian retail: mandating scripts increased sales 5.6%
- 12% of world is WEIRD — Western, Educated, Industrialized, Rich, Democratic
- 96% of psych studies come from that 12%
- "Give people ownership" literally harms performance in some contexts

---

### 4. WHAT TO DO INSTEAD 

1. **Screen differently.** Ask: "Harshest feedback you've received — what did you do?" Then give real feedback mid-interview. Watch the reaction.

2. **Calibrate by stakes.** Low-risk → 50%. Standard → 70%. Irreversible → 90%.

3. **Ask the golden question.** "Would detailed direction or developing your own approach be more helpful?"

---

### 5. THE CLOSE 

**Callback, define the metaphor, land the takeaways.**

- Those 82% of managers knew what to hold on to — they just let go of it
- "Hire the metabolism, not the resume" — metabolism means the speed at which someone digests new information, recovers from mistakes, and converts feedback into growth. Not what they know today, but how fast they learn tomorrow.
- Three takeaways on your cheat sheet:
  1. **Who > What** — who you delegate to matters more than which framework you use
  2. **Tools, Not Rules** — the 70% Rule, RACI, SLII have zero empirical validation; use as thinking aids, not gospel
  3. **Context Is King** — more autonomy can backfire; match your style to the person, culture, and stakes
- "The science of letting go is less about letting go and more about knowing what to hold on to."

❯ what are RACI, SLII?

⏺ Both are popular management frameworks referenced in the episode's evidence void — widely used but lacking empirical validation:

  RACI — A role-assignment matrix used in project management. For any task or decision, you label each person as:
  - Responsible (does the work)
  - Accountable (owns the outcome)
  - Consulted (gives input before)
  - Informed (told after)

  Developed in the 1950s. Used heavily in IT, construction, finance. McKinsey identified 4 "major pitfalls" in 2022 and proposed an alternative (DARE) — also unvalidated. No peer-reviewed study has ever shown
  teams using RACI outperform teams that don't.

  SLII — Situational Leadership II, created by Ken Blanchard. Prescribes matching your leadership style to the person's development level:
  - D1 (low competence, high commitment) → S1 Directing
  - D2 (some competence, low commitment) → S2 Coaching
  - D3 (high competence, variable commitment) → S3 Supporting
  - D4 (high competence, high commitment) → S4 Delegating

  Both frameworks are useful as thinking prompts — they ask the right questions. The problem is treating them as proven science when neither has been tested in a controlled experiment.
