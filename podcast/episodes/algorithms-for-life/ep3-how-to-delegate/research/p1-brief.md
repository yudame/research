# Research Brief: Algorithms for Life: Ep. 3, How to Delegate

**Date:** 2025-12-27
**Episode:** Algorithms for Life: Ep. 3, How to Delegate
**Series:** Algorithms for Life

## Research Topic

Research the psychology, economics, and systems of effective delegation for high-competence entrepreneurs scaling beyond solo operation. Investigate why skilled founders struggle to delegate, what research reveals about expertise transfer and training ROI, and what frameworks enable successful handoffs without sacrificing quality.

## Key Questions

### The Competence Trap Psychology
- What psychological factors make high-competence individuals resist delegation?
- What does research show about the "founder's dilemma" of doing vs. managing?
- How does expertise create blind spots about what's actually transferable?

### The Economics of Delegation
- What frameworks exist for calculating delegation ROI?
- What does research show about the true cost of founder bottlenecks on business growth?
- At what thresholds does delegation become mathematically necessary?

### Expertise Transfer and Tacit Knowledge
- What does cognitive science reveal about tacit knowledge transfer?
- How do experts successfully transfer expertise?
- What role does feedback quality play in accelerating trainee competence?

### Hiring and Selection
- What predicts trainability and growth potential better than current skill level?
- How do you identify candidates who can eventually match founder competence?

### Delegation Frameworks and Gradual Handoff
- What delegation frameworks exist in management literature?
- What does research show about gradual vs. full handoff approaches?
- How do you delegate judgment and decision-making, not just tasks?

### The Manager Transition
- How do successful entrepreneurs navigate identity shifts from doer to manager?
- What's the research on "letting them fail" as necessary for delegation?

### Systems Thinking for Scale
- What does operations research reveal about standardization and process design?
- How do franchise models achieve consistent quality through delegation?

## Context

This episode targets the skilled entrepreneur who has built competence through years of hands-on execution. They can do most things better than anyone they could afford to hire, and the time required to train someone feels prohibitive. The result: they remain the bottleneck, unable to scale beyond their personal capacity.

The core tension: short-term efficiency (do it yourself) vs. long-term leverage (invest in others). The research question is whether there are systematic approaches that reduce training time investment while maintaining quality—or whether the math simply requires accepting a temporary quality/efficiency drop as the price of scale.
