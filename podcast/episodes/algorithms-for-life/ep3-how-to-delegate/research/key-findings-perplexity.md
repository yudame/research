# Key Findings from Perplexity Research

## Standout Data Points

### Learning Agility (Strongest Evidence)
- **ρ = 0.74** correlation with leader performance
- **ρ = 0.75** correlation with leadership potential
- **r = 0.09** correlation with general intelligence (virtually none!)
- **r = -0.22** "developing leadership" dimension negatively correlates with IQ
- Learning agility correlates with achievement motivation (r = 0.54 thoughts, r = 0.47 behaviors)

**Implication:** Hire for learning agility, not IQ or experience. This is counterintuitive and powerful.

### The 70% Rule (The Big Gap!)
- **Zero peer-reviewed empirical validation** despite widespread adoption
- Created by Jim Schleckser based on consulting experience with 2,500-3,000 CEOs
- Appears in Inc.com 2014 and "Great CEOs Are Lazy" book
- Functions as practitioner heuristic, not research-backed guideline

**Implication:** Perfect episode hook - "Everyone cites this rule, but it has no research backing"

### New Hire Failures (Attitudinal > Technical)
- **46%** of new hires fail within 18 months
- **89%** of failures are attitudinal (not technical!)
- **82%** of hiring managers saw warning signs but ignored them
- Breakdown: 26% coachability, 23% emotional intelligence, 17% motivation, 15% temperament, 11% technical skills
- Only **19%** achieve unequivocal success

**Implication:** Technical competence is least predictive of delegation success

### Knowledge Transfer Mechanisms
- Toyota deployed **200-300 experienced workers for months** to transfer production system to France
- Tacit knowledge requires social networks, personnel mobility, observation
- Explicit knowledge transfers via documentation, training systems
- Mentoring predicts tacit knowledge transfer (β = 0.806, p < 0.001)
- Job crafting mediates mentoring effectiveness (β = 0.627, p < 0.001)

**Implication:** You can't document expertise - need structured transfer protocols

### Situational Leadership (Mixed Validation)
- SLII model: 4 development levels (D1-D4) matched to 4 styles (S1-S4)
- **9 out of 10 outcomes** showed fit between needed/received style predicted success
- BUT: "Dearth of solid empirical evidence" according to critical analysis
- Diagnosis step (determining D1-D4) lacks reliability research

**Implication:** Popular framework but weaker empirical foundation than claimed

### Delegation Benefits
- Teams with effective delegation: **33% productivity increase**
- Delegation predicts feedback-seeking (β = 0.31, p < 0.001)
- Psychological empowerment mediates delegation effectiveness

## Counterpoint Opportunities

1. **70% Rule:** Practical vs. Empirical
   - Speaker A: "Works in practice across thousands of CEOs"
   - Speaker B: "But zero controlled studies - could be confirmation bias"

2. **Learning Agility vs. IQ:** Counterintuitive finding
   - Speaker A: "This explains why smart people often fail at delegation"
   - Speaker B: "But how do we measure learning agility reliably in hiring?"

3. **Situational Leadership:** Adoption vs. Validation
   - Speaker A: "Thousands of organizations use SLII successfully"
   - Speaker B: "Yet academic consensus is evidence is thin"

4. **New Hire Failures:** Observable but Ignored
   - Speaker A: "82% saw warning signs - this is about discipline"
   - Speaker B: "Or maybe our interview methods can't actually assess attitude"
