## Phase 2: Perplexity Academic Research (Completed)

**Prompt used:**
```
Research the psychology and decision science of strategic selection under conditions of opportunity abundance, examining why visionary, possibility-oriented thinkers struggle with long-horizon strategy execution despite strong creative output.

**Research methodology:**
- Prioritize peer-reviewed studies, meta-analyses, systematic reviews, and authoritative sources
- Distinguish between correlation and causation in findings
- Report effect sizes and practical significance, not just statistical significance
- Note the study populations and whether findings generalize to relevant demographics
- Compare individual studies against meta-analyses and systematic reviews
- Identify preliminary research vs. well-replicated findings
- Note funding sources and potential conflicts of interest when relevant
- Include contradictory findings and areas of scientific uncertainty
- Cite specific studies, researchers, and sources throughout
- Provide full source URLs for all citations

**Key areas to cover:**
1. Cognitive psychology of decision-making with multiple high-value, long-horizon opportunities (6-18 month initiatives)
2. Opportunity cost psychology when comparing initiatives with different time horizons and risk profiles
3. Cognitive load and context-switching effects on strategic execution
4. Analysis paralysis versus premature commitment - optimal decision points
5. Personality psychology (Big Five, MBTI research validity) on strategic planning vs idea generation differences
6. High-openness, high-extraversion individuals and executive function, sustained attention, delayed gratification
7. Novelty-seeking as both strength (innovation) and liability (abandonment)
8. Decision frameworks for comparing 2-5 major initiatives with incommensurable benefits
9. Making long-term trade-offs "legible" (visible, concrete, emotionally salient)
10. Minimum viable structure that increases follow-through without crushing intrinsic motivation
11. Implementation intentions and "if-then" planning effectiveness
12. Accountability structures (coaches, peers, public commitments) and completion rates

**Output:** Comprehensive research report with extensive citations, sample sizes, methodological details, and source links.
```

**Result:** ~9,200 words with 84 citations covering all 12 areas. Saved to research/p2-perplexity.md

## Phase 3: Question Discovery

**After analyzing Perplexity's academic research, here are the questions we should investigate:**

### What subtopics and themes emerged?
- **Extensively covered:** Opportunity cost psychology, analysis paralysis/choice overload, Big Five personality traits, novelty-seeking neurobiology, implementation intentions, accountability structures, context switching costs, construal level theory
- **Moderate coverage:** Minimum viable structure, temporal motivation theory, psychological safety
- **Brief mentions:** Interest-based nervous system (ADHD lens), pre-commitment strategies, goal revision after failure

### What gaps exist in the academic literature?
- Limited research on AI-assisted decision-making for strategic selection
- Few studies on how successful multi-project entrepreneurs actually manage attention
- Minimal research on optimal number of parallel initiatives (2 vs 3 vs 5)
- Lack of longitudinal studies on visionary leaders who overcame execution challenges

### What recent developments aren't covered?
- AI tools for decision-making and strategic planning (2023-2025)
- Post-COVID changes in executive attention and remote decision-making
- New research on ADHD medication effects on strategic execution
- Emerging frameworks from executive coaching industry

### What contradictions or uncertainties need more sources?
- Ego depletion vs. opportunity cost models of mental effort (ongoing debate)
- Optimal level of structure for creative personalities (tension between studies)
- Whether MBTI has any validity vs. Big Five exclusively
- How much accountability is optimal before it becomes demotivating

### What industry/implementation questions arose?
- What specific AI workflows exist for initiative comparison?
- How do successful entrepreneurs structure their decision rhythms?
- What executive coaching interventions show strongest evidence?
- Case studies of companies managing multiple strategic bets

### What policy/regulatory angles need investigation?
- Not directly applicable to this topic

### What practitioner perspectives are missing?
- Executive coaches on what actually works with ENFP-type clients
- Entrepreneurs on how they maintain focus across ventures
- Productivity experts on AI-augmented strategic planning
- Neurodivergent professionals on adapting systems to different brains

- Conduct comprehensive research across academic, industry, and practitioner sources
