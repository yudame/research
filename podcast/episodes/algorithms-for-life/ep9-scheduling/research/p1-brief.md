# Episode 9: What to Do First

## Research Prompt

Research scheduling algorithms as frameworks for personal productivity and time management. Investigate the major scheduling strategies from computer science — earliest deadline first, shortest job first, weighted shortest job first, priority scheduling, round-robin, and preemptive vs. non-preemptive approaches — and how each maps to real-life task management. Explore the key tradeoffs: minimizing lateness vs. maximizing throughput vs. reducing context-switching overhead. Examine the research on priority inversion (when low-priority tasks block high-priority ones), thrashing (when you spend more time switching than working), and interrupt coalescing (batching interruptions). Investigate how these concepts relate to modern productivity challenges: email, meetings, deep work, and multitasking. Explore what operations research and queuing theory reveal about why some people feel perpetually behind despite working constantly. Examine the computational complexity of optimal scheduling (often NP-hard) and what that implies about the futility of finding a "perfect" system.
