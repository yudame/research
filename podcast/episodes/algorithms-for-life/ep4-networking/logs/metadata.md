## Description (Plain Text for RSS <description>)

What can computer networking protocols teach us about human communication? Discover how your brain's 10-bit-per-second bottleneck shapes every conversation, why exponential backoff is "the algorithm of forgiveness" for flaky friends, and the counterintuitive science showing that lossy communication often beats lossless precision. From TCP handshakes hiding in your phone greetings to Walmart's billion-dollar protocol mismatch in Germany, this episode maps the hidden parallels between network engineering and human connection.

Full research report: https://research.yuda.me/podcast/episodes/algorithms-for-life/ep4-networking/report.md

- Why your brain processes only 10 bits per second through a billion-bit firehose — and how to work with this bottleneck instead of against it

- What a billion-dollar protocol mismatch at Walmart Germany teaches about cross-cultural communication failures
- Why experts make better decisions using "lossy" gist-based thinking than precise verbatim details (Fuzzy Trace Theory)

- **Zheng & Meister (2025) - "The Unbearable Slowness of Being"**: Landmark paper proving conscious thought operates at ~10 bits per second
  - URL: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0896627324008080
  - Use this to: Understand why communication bottlenecks are biological, not personal failings

- **Coupé et al. (2019) - Universal language rate**: Cross-linguistic study showing all 17 languages converge on ~39 bits/second
  - Use this to: Appreciate the species-wide speed limit on verbal communication

- **Stivers et al. (2009) - Turn-taking universals**: PNAS study confirming conversation turn-taking is near-universal across 17 languages
  - Use this to: Understand why conversation protocols are innate, not learned

- **Reyna & Brainerd - Fuzzy Trace Theory**: Research showing gist-based (lossy) processing outperforms verbatim for expert decision-making
