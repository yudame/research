# Research Brief: Algorithms for Life: Ep. 1, Spaced Repetition

**Date:** 2025-12-15
**Episode:** Algorithms for Life: Ep. 1, Spaced Repetition - The Algorithm That Optimizes How We Remember

---

## Research Topic

This episode explores spaced repetition as an algorithmic approach to learning and memory optimization. We'll examine the neuroscience foundations of the forgetting curve and memory consolidation, how algorithms like SM-2 and modern variants (FSRS, Half-Life Regression) translate these biological mechanisms into scheduling systems, and how mobile technology has transformed spaced repetition practice into mainstream adoption.

The research will investigate the complete ecosystem of spaced repetition systems (SRS), from the highly customizable Anki to the gamified Duolingo, examining their design philosophies, algorithmic implementations, and evidence for effectiveness. Critically, we'll explore the limitations and context problems inherent in isolated flashcard learning, particularly the recognition versus production gap and transfer challenges to real-world communication.

## Key Questions

- What are the biological mechanisms underlying the forgetting curve and memory consolidation at the neural level?
- How do SM-2 and modern algorithms (FSRS, Half-Life Regression) work, and what are their trade-offs between simplicity and optimization?
- How has mobile access transformed SRS practice, and what UX patterns (notifications, streaks, microlearning) affect learning effectiveness?
- What are the design philosophies and trade-offs of major apps (Anki, Duolingo, Memrise, Clozemaster, LingQ)?
- What are the critical limitations including the recognition vs production gap, context-dependent memory, and transfer failure?
- How can SRS be integrated with comprehensible input and output practice for optimal learning?
- What domains beyond language learning successfully use spaced repetition?

## Context

This is the first episode in the "Algorithms for Life" series, examining systematic approaches to human optimization. Spaced repetition represents a clear case where understanding biological mechanisms (memory consolidation, forgetting curves) enables algorithmic interventions that dramatically improve outcomes.

The episode will balance celebration of what spaced repetition does well (efficient retention of facts and vocabulary) with honest examination of its limitations (context dependence, production gap, transfer problems). The goal is to help listeners understand both when SRS is the right tool and when it needs to be complemented with other approaches.
