### Phase 1: Perplexity (Academic Foundation)

**Submitted:** 2026-02-11
**Mode:** sonar-deep-research (async API)

```
Research two counterintuitive problem-solving strategies from computer science — relaxation and randomness — and their applications to human decision-making. For RELAXATION: investigate linear programming relaxation, Lagrangian relaxation, and constraint relaxation as formal techniques for solving NP-hard optimization problems. How do these methods work mathematically? What are their approximation guarantees? Explore published research on applying constraint relaxation to real-life decisions — career planning, scheduling, negotiation, resource allocation. Include work by Brian Christian, Tom Griffiths ("Algorithms to Live By"), and any cognitive science research on the benefits of temporarily ignoring constraints when problem-solving. For RANDOMNESS: investigate randomized algorithms (Las Vegas vs Monte Carlo types), simulated annealing, random restarts, and stochastic optimization. What are the theoretical and empirical results showing when randomized approaches outperform deterministic ones? Examine research on random sampling vs exhaustive search, satisficing vs maximizing (Herbert Simon), and the explore-exploit tradeoff's connection to randomness. Include psychological research on analysis paralysis, overchoice, and whether introducing randomness into personal decision-making improves outcomes. For the CONNECTION between both: explore how both strategies involve deliberately letting go of rigid optimization to find better solutions, and any unified theoretical frameworks. Include research on local vs global optima in both computational and human decision-making contexts.

**Research methodology:**
- Prioritize peer-reviewed studies, meta-analyses, systematic reviews, and authoritative sources
- Distinguish between correlation and causation in findings
- Report effect sizes and practical significance, not just statistical significance
- Note the study populations and whether findings generalize to relevant demographics
- Compare individual studies against meta-analyses and systematic reviews
- Identify preliminary research vs. well-replicated findings
- Note funding sources and potential conflicts of interest when relevant
- Include contradictory findings and areas of scientific uncertainty
- Cite specific studies, researchers, and sources throughout
- Provide full source URLs for all citations

**Output:** Comprehensive research report with extensive citations, sample sizes, methodological details, and source links.
```

### Phase 3: Claude (Comprehensive Synthesis)

**Submitted:** 2026-02-11
**Mode:** Manual (user pastes at https://claude.ai)

```
Research the real-world applications and human stories behind two computer science strategies: relaxation (solving hard problems by loosening constraints) and randomness (when random approaches beat deliberation). Focus on these specific questions:
1. What are the most compelling real-world stories of people or organizations solving "impossible" problems by relaxing constraints? Include: NASA mission planning, military logistics, startup pivots, or any domain where someone said "what if we ignored that rule?" and found a breakthrough. Cite specific examples with names, dates, and outcomes.
2. How does simulated annealing map to human life transitions? Research evidence on whether temporarily accepting worse situations (pay cuts for career changes, relationship disruption for growth, moving cities) leads to better long-term outcomes. Include longitudinal studies on career changers, second-act entrepreneurs, and "strategic quitting."
3. What does the research show about randomness and creativity? Investigate serendipity research, the role of chance in scientific discovery (Fleming's penicillin, Post-it Notes, etc.), brainstorming techniques that introduce randomness, and whether deliberately random exploration improves creative output.
4. What are the limits and failures of these approaches? When does relaxation go too far (removing too many constraints)? When does randomness fail (domains requiring precision)? What are the boundary conditions?
5. What practical frameworks exist for applying these concepts? Step-by-step methods someone could use tomorrow: "constraint inventory" exercises, random coffee meetings, sabbatical planning, decision coin-flips.
**Research methodology:**
- Conduct comprehensive research across academic, industry, policy, and recent sources
- Prioritize authoritative sources and distinguish correlation from causation
- Note methodological limitations and conflicts of interest
- Include contradictory findings and areas of uncertainty
- Cite specific studies, reports, and sources extensively with URLs
```
