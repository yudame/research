## Description (Plain Text for RSS <description>)

Gene Kranz didn't care what the hose was designed to do — he cared what it could do. That Apollo 13 moment launches a deep dive into constraint relaxation and strategic randomness, two computer science strategies that explain why the most successful pivots in history (Slack's $27.7B exit, Voyager's gravity assist, JWST's folding mirror) all required deliberately breaking the rules. But the same principle that saved Apollo 13 killed 346 people on the Boeing 737 MAX, revealing a critical asymmetry: individuals are systematically too cautious, while institutions are systematically too reckless. Includes Levitt's 22,500-person coin-flip study, myth-busts on the jam study and decision fatigue, and five practical protocols for knowing when to let go.

Full research report: https://research.bwforce.ai/podcast/episodes/algorithms-for-life/ep6-relaxation-randomness/report.md

## What You'll Learn

- How "half a camera" solves impossible problems — the counterintuitive math of LP relaxation and why breaking reality fixes reality
- Why people who flip a coin and make a change are 2.2 points happier on a 10-point scale six months later (Levitt, N=22,500)
- The jam study is wrong: a 50-experiment meta-analysis found the effect size of choice overload is virtually zero
- Why decision fatigue isn't about glucose — the replication crisis busted the willpower-as-fuel model (Hagger 2016, 23 labs)
- The asymmetry framework: individuals need to be pushed to let go, institutions need to be forced to hold on

## Resources & Tools Mentioned

### Research Papers & Meta-Analyses
- **Levitt 2020 - Coin Flip Experiment**: People who made changes were 2.2 points happier at 6 months (N=22,500)
  - URL: https://gwern.net/doc/psychology/cognitive-bias/2020-levitt.pdf
  - Use this to: Overcome status quo bias when you're genuinely stuck on a major life decision

- **Scheibehenne, Greifeneder & Todd 2010 - Choice Overload Meta-Analysis**: Effect size ~0 across 50 experiments
  - Use this to: Stop artificially restricting options — choice overload is context-dependent, not universal

- **Hagger et al. 2016 - Ego Depletion RRR**: Failed to replicate across 23 labs, N=2,000+
  - Use this to: Replace Snickers breaks with context switches — decision fatigue is about motivation, not glucose

- **Schwartz - Maximization Scale**: Maximizers earn more but correlate with regret at r>0.50
  - Use this to: Understand why optimization is great for bridges but toxic for happiness

- **Rajkumar et al. 2022 - LinkedIn Weak Ties**: Moderately weak ties maximize job mobility (N=20M)
  - URL: https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.abl4476
  - Use this to: Prioritize acquaintance networking over close-friend referrals for career opportunities

- **Theory of Constraints (Goldratt)**: Label constraints as physics, legal, or self-imposed — then test one self-imposed constraint
  - Use this to: Identify which rules in your life are load-bearing and which are just expectations
