# Episode 10: The Final Move

## Research Prompt

Research game theory as a framework for understanding strategic interactions in everyday life. Investigate the foundational concepts — Nash equilibrium, dominant strategies, the prisoner's dilemma, and coordination games — and how they explain behavior in negotiations, relationships, and group decision-making. Explore mechanism design (the "reverse game theory" that designs rules to produce desired outcomes) and its applications in auctions, voting systems, matching markets, and incentive structures. Examine the price of anarchy — how much efficiency is lost when individuals act selfishly vs. cooperatively — and what it reveals about traffic, shared resources, and organizational design. Investigate repeated games, tit-for-tat, and how cooperation emerges (or collapses) over time. Explore information asymmetry (signaling, screening, adverse selection) in job markets, dating, and negotiation. Examine what game theory gets wrong about human behavior and where behavioral game theory fills the gaps. Consider how this serves as a capstone for the series: when other minds enter the equation, all the previous algorithms must be reconsidered.
