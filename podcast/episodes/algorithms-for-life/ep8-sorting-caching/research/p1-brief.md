# Episode 8: The Art of Organization

## Research Prompt

Research sorting algorithms and caching as complementary frameworks for organizing life. For sorting: investigate why sorting is computationally expensive (O(n log n) lower bound), what different sorting algorithms (quicksort, mergesort, bucket sort, radix sort) reveal about different organizing strategies, and critically — when it's actually better NOT to sort at all. Explore the concept of "sort on demand" vs. "pre-sort everything," how search costs trade off against sorting costs, and what this means for organizing physical spaces, email inboxes, and priorities. For caching: research how computer memory hierarchies (L1/L2/L3 cache, RAM, disk) explain why forgetting is a feature not a bug. Investigate LRU (least recently used), LFU (least frequently used), and other eviction policies as models for what to keep close at hand. Explore how caching principles apply to organizing your desk, closet, kitchen, and digital files. Examine the connections: both are about managing the cost of retrieval — sorting reduces search time, caching reduces access time, and both reveal that perfect organization is often worse than "good enough."
