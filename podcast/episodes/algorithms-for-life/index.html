Ep 1 Spaced Repetition - The Algorithm That Optimizes How We Remember 43:38 Report Transcript
Despite 140 years of research showing 74% better retention than cramming, only 0.1% of Duolingo's 500 million users complete a course. The paradox: a technique with molecular mechanisms and laboratory proof that collapses when it meets real behavior.
More details
Explores the molecular biology: CREB protein as the switch determining temporary vs. lasting memory, fruit fly experiments showing 7-day vs. 3-day retention from identical training with different spacing. Covers the algorithmic evolution from Wozniak's 1985 SM-0 to modern FSRS with 21 trainable parameters optimized via machine learning. Details the Cepeda meta-analysis of 839 assessments showing optimal intervals are proportional to retention goals (20-40% for one-week, 5-10% for one-year). Investigates the recognition-production chasm: why learners with 20,000+ reviewed cards struggle with basic conversation. Examines context-dependent memory research showing 40% performance drops when retrieval context differs from learning context. Reveals that sophisticated algorithms produce only ~3% better outcomes than fixed intervals, suggesting the algorithm is not the bottleneck. Includes integration strategies with comprehensible input and deliberate output practice.
