## Contradictions & Discrepancies

### 1. Massage Gun Market Size
- **GPT-Researcher:** $694M (2024) via 360iResearch
- **Grok:** $3.7B by 2035 via Future Market Insights
- **Analysis:** Different baselines suggest different category definitions (massage guns only vs broader massage equipment)
- **Resolution:** Both indicate strong growth; use narrower definition ($694M→$1.21B) for massage guns specifically

### 2. Traditional vs Infrared Sauna Effects
- **Perplexity:** Traditional sauna may harm performance (swimmers), infrared may help (team sports)
- **Other sources:** No direct comparison data
- **Analysis:** Sport-specific and protocol-specific effects; no head-to-head comparison
- **Resolution:** Flag as "preliminary, needs replication" - different physiological stress profiles

### 3. CWI in Elite Athletes
- **Claude:** Elite athletes (Super Rugby, pro soccer) show no strength impairment despite CWI
- **Claude:** Lab studies consistently show impairment in recreationally active populations
- **Analysis:** Ecological validity issue or training status moderation effect
- **Resolution:** Context-dependent - elite vs recreational may respond differently OR higher training volumes compensate

## Confidence Assessment

### High Confidence (✅ Multiple sources, meta-analyses, large N)
1. Stretching has minimal-to-zero effect on DOMS recovery (Cochrane, multiple meta-analyses)
2. CWI reduces DOMS acutely (multiple meta-analyses, consistent effects)
3. CWI impairs hypertrophy adaptations in resistance training (Roberts 2015 + subsequent replications)
4. Foam rolling reduces DOMS (consistent across sources)
5. 48-72h recovery periods recommended for 40+ adults (NSCA, practitioner consensus)

### Moderate Confidence (⚠️ Single strong source OR replicated but smaller studies)
1. Optimal CWI protocol: 10-15 min at 11-15°C (network meta-analysis but heterogeneity)
2. Infrared sauna superior to traditional for neuromuscular recovery (limited direct comparisons)
3. Massage timing: 48-72h optimal (logical but limited direct studies)
4. Massage gun market growth trajectory (market research, not peer-reviewed)

### Low Confidence (⚡ Preliminary, needs replication)
1. Specific genetic variants explain recovery variation (modest explanatory power: 4.1%)
2. CWI placebo effect accounts for substantial benefit (emerging evidence)
3. Duration > roller type for foam rolling effectiveness (practical but not rigorously tested)
4. 89.3% athlete stretching usage rate (survey but single source)

---

## Gaps Requiring Additional Research

1. **Age-specific protocols:** Most studies use 20-30 year olds; limited data comparing 35-50 age group recovery
2. **Multimodal integration:** How to optimally combine heat, cold, stretching, massage - no rigorous studies
3. **Individual variation mechanisms:** Beyond "genetics" - what predicts responder status?
4. **Long-term repeated use:** Most studies are acute (24-96h); chronic adaptation unclear
5. **Direct sauna comparisons:** Traditional vs infrared head-to-head with adequate power
6. **Cost-effectiveness analysis:** DIY recovery (foam rolling) vs professional services (massage therapy) - limited economic analysis
