## Notes for Research Briefing

**Strongest evidence for:**
- Active recovery improves acute lactate clearance and perceived exertion
- Sleep and nutrition are foundational, cost-effective recovery methods
- Master athletes (40+) don't need more protein per kg than younger athletes
- Compression therapy and cryotherapy show small-medium benefits with high cost
- Individual variability dominates - no universal optimal protocol

**Weaker evidence for:**
- Specific product recommendations (limited to review sites)
- Exact pricing (market-dependent, 2025 US data)
- Professional team protocols (anecdotal, industry reporting)
- Circadian timing specifics (limited sources, though high quality)

**Interesting tensions/contradictions:**
- Anabolic window debate: flexible extended window vs traditional timing
- Active recovery: strong acute benefits, unclear long-term advantage
- Compression/cryo: market growth vs modest evidence base
- Cost vs efficacy: expensive tech offers marginal gains over fundamentals

**Missing context:**
- Long-term longitudinal studies on master athlete recovery
- Head-to-head comparisons of recovery modalities in 40+ population
- Cost-benefit analysis with quality-adjusted life years (QALYs)
