## Research Output

# Social-Emotional Learning in Early Childhood: Evidence-Based Strategies for Microschool Implementation

**The most effective SEL approach for a microschool with children ages 4-6 combines brief daily explicit instruction (minimum 15 minutes) with integration throughout the day—and new research suggests post-pandemic cohorts require more intensive support than pre-2020 baselines.** Meta-analyses consistently show that programs meeting CASEL's SAFE criteria (Sequenced, Active, Focused, Explicit) and delivered by teachers rather than external specialists generate the strongest outcomes, with effect sizes of **0.15-0.57** across social-emotional competencies. Microschools hold a structural advantage: low ratios enable the real-time coaching and relationship depth that evidence identifies as essential for ages 4-6, though direct research on microschool-specific SEL outcomes remains limited.

Three converging evidence streams inform this report: pandemic-impact research documenting **15-25%** increases in social-emotional delays among young children; meta-analyses comparing instructional approaches across **575,000+ students**; and practitioner documentation from micro-school networks serving **10,000+ children**. The synthesis reveals both strong consensus (combining explicit and integrated instruction works best) and genuine uncertainty (optimal dosage thresholds, long-term pandemic recovery trajectories, and whether microschool contexts amplify or merely sustain program effects).

## Pandemic impacts on young children demand SEL attention through 2026

Children who were ages 4-6 during 2020-2021 represent a particularly vulnerable cohort, with mounting evidence suggesting they face persistent developmental challenges that older pandemic-era students are recovering from more quickly.

A **September 2024 PLOS Global Public Health scoping review** synthesizing 13 studies representing **956,436 children** identified four major deficit themes: lack of socialization opportunities (difficulties with sharing, cooperative play, turn-taking), emotional regression (challenges with self-regulation and conflict resolution), cognitive gaps (attention and concentration difficulties), and inequitable impacts concentrated among children with disabilities and low-SES backgrounds. The American Journal of Preventive Medicine published findings in 2025 from **25,050 children** tracked via Ages & Stages Questionnaires, showing that socioemotional delay risk increased significantly starting two years post-pandemic-onset, with effects most pronounced for children in vulnerable environments.

**The youngest learners are not catching up.** Curriculum Associates' 2024 analysis found that students who were in preschool/kindergarten during pandemic closures remain below pre-pandemic developmental benchmarks, while older elementary students show accelerated recovery. Researchers noted being "shocked" by these findings—the toll on foundational social-emotional development appears more persistent than anticipated. A cross-national meta-analysis of **22,348 children across 16 countries** confirmed small but significant increases in emotional symptoms, conduct problems, and anxiety, with effects detected using standardized instruments (SDQ, CBCL) in pre/post pandemic evaluations.

**Specific deficits documented in ages 4-6 include:**
- Fewer reciprocal friendships and heightened anxiety around new peers
- Regression to earlier developmental stages for emotional regulation
- Loss of age-appropriate coping strategies
- Reduced emotion recognition accuracy (potentially linked to mask-wearing during critical development windows)
- Higher hyperactivity and peer problems among children who experienced remote instruction

These findings carry direct implications for microschool practice: SEL programming designed for 2019 baselines may prove insufficient for children entering school in 2023-2026. Explicit attention to foundational skills—emotion vocabulary, basic turn-taking, self-calming strategies—may need extended timelines compared to pre-pandemic cohorts.

## CASEL's framework evolution emphasizes equity and adult well-being

The field's central organizing body, CASEL, substantially updated its framework in 2020, adding explicit focus on **educational equity and establishing equitable learning environments** across four settings (classrooms, schools, families, communities). This update introduced "Transformative SEL," defined as "a process whereby young people and adults build strong, respectful, and lasting relationships that facilitate co-learning to critically examine root causes of inequity."

**Adoption has accelerated dramatically.** RAND/CASEL tracking data shows:
- **2017-18**: 46% of U.S. schools reported using SEL curricula
- **2021-22**: 76% reported usage
- **2023-24**: 83% reported usage

Forty-nine states now have supportive SEL policies. The instructional materials market reached **$1.725 billion in 2022** (25.9% year-over-year growth), with digital programs accounting for 65% of SEL-related sales.

CASEL's July 2020 "Reunite, Renew, and Thrive" roadmap provided COVID-specific reopening guidance, emphasizing three priority areas: promoting SEL for students, building adult SEL capacity, and establishing school-family-community partnerships. This adult focus represents a meaningful shift—the field increasingly recognizes that teacher stress and burnout undermine SEL delivery regardless of curriculum quality. RAND's 2024 survey found **53% of principals** cite insufficient training as a significant implementation barrier.

State-level innovation has also emerged. Oregon's 2023 Transformative SEL Framework and Standards, mandated by House Bill 2166, represents the most comprehensive state adoption, with district implementation required by July 2024. The framework explicitly integrates equity considerations into developmental competencies.

## Meta-analyses reveal optimal instructional approaches

### Effect sizes favor structured, teacher-led programs

The evidence base for SEL effectiveness is substantial, though effect sizes are modest. **Cipriano et al.'s 2023 meta-analysis**—the most comprehensive contemporary synthesis—analyzed **424 studies, 53 countries, and 575,361 students**:

| Outcome Domain | Effect Size (g) |
|----------------|-----------------|
| SEL skills | 0.178 |
| Peer relationships | 0.267 |
| Attitudes/beliefs | 0.200 |
| Emotional distress | 0.122 |
| Disruptive behaviors | 0.220 |

The foundational **Durlak et al. (2011) meta-analysis** of 213 programs and **270,034 students** found stronger effects: SEL skills (ES = 0.57), positive social behavior (ES = 0.24), and an **11 percentile-point gain** in academic achievement. Teacher-implemented programs showed substantially larger effects (ES = 0.34 on academic performance) than those delivered by non-school personnel (ES = 0.12).

**Taylor et al.'s 2017 follow-up effects meta-analysis** tracked outcomes from 6 months to 18 years post-intervention, finding sustained academic achievement effects of **ES = 0.26-0.33**. Crucially, post-intervention social-emotional skill development was the strongest predictor of later well-being—suggesting that what matters is whether SEL actually builds skills, not merely whether programs are delivered.

### The SAFE criteria predict program success

Programs meeting all four SAFE criteria show significantly stronger effects:
- **Sequenced**: Connected, coordinated activities building skills over time
- **Active**: Hands-on, experiential learning rather than didactic instruction
- **Focused**: Intentional attention to developing specific SEL competencies
- **Explicit**: Students know what skills they're learning and why

This finding has direct implications for microschool implementation: informal, emergent SEL approaches may feel natural in intimate settings but likely underperform structured curricula that meet SAFE criteria.

### Explicit instruction provides the foundation; integration provides practice

CASEL explicitly recommends **combining dedicated and integrated instruction**. Their three-component model identifies: supportive classroom climate + explicit SEL instruction + integration into academic content. Research does not support replacing one approach with the other.

**Cipriano et al. (2023) identified a critical sequencing finding**: programs that teach **intrapersonal skills first** (self-awareness, self-management) followed by **interpersonal skills** (social awareness, relationships) produce stronger outcomes than the reverse. This suggests explicit instruction should establish foundational self-regulation before emphasizing peer interactions.

The Head Start REDI study provides compelling evidence that integration alone is insufficient. While the program combined SEL with literacy, researchers found that language/literacy gains alone **did not predict improved behavioral adjustment**—explicit SEL targeting was specifically required for social-behavioral gains. Cross-domain effects emerged: SEL gains during preschool made unique contributions to both academic AND behavioral outcomes in kindergarten.

**Literacy emerges as the strongest academic domain for SEL integration.** Fisher and Frey (2019) identify it as the content area best suited to SEL embedding, and programs like 4Rs (Reading, Writing, Respect, Resolution) demonstrate effective literacy-SEL synthesis through character analysis and perspective-taking in children's literature.

### Dosage research remains imprecise but informative

Evidence supports a **minimum of 75 minutes per week** (approximately 15 minutes daily) for explicit instruction, based on recommendations from CASEL and Maurice Elias. A recent meta-analysis found that "low program dosage" is significantly less effective than approaching recommended dosage, though precise thresholds remain undefined.

**Practical findings on dosage:**
- 15 minutes twice weekly showed noticeable impact on student self-awareness and relationships (Corona-Norco USD, 2024)
- Higher program dosage correlates with stronger effects on socio-emotional functioning
- CASEL recommends dedicated time in the master schedule, with explicit instruction during advisory or the first 30 minutes of homeroom

For a microschool context, this translates to daily brief instruction (5-15 minutes depending on program) plus consistent integration touchpoints (morning meeting, transitions, conflict moments, closing circle).

## Age-specific evidence for children ages 4-6

**Blewitt et al.'s 2018 meta-analysis** specifically examined curriculum-based SEL for children ages 2-6 across 79 studies, finding small to moderate effects on social-emotional competence, behavioral self-regulation, emotional/behavioral problems, and early academic performance. Notably, SEL programs administered at **relatively low intensity may be effective** for increasing social competence in this age group—high-touch, brief daily engagement can work.

**Murano et al.'s 2020 preschool meta-analysis** of 48 studies found that targeted (selective) programs showed larger effect sizes than universal programs for this age group, suggesting that children showing early difficulties benefit most from intervention intensity.

Research identifies **key developmental considerations for ages 4-6:**
- Prefrontal cortex undergoing rapid development; executive function improving
- Emotional vocabulary expanding from 2-3 basic emotions toward 5-7
- Transition from parallel play to collaborative play
- Heavy reliance on adult co-regulation; independent self-regulation is emerging, not established
- Learning best through concrete, hands-on experiences rather than abstract discussion

Boys are identified as a vulnerable subgroup requiring careful attention to SEL needs in this age range, with weaker SEL profiles at preschool entry predicting combined risks in both academic and social domains.

## Microschool implementation: documented practices and practical frameworks

### Named microschool networks offer tested approaches

**Acton Academy** (300+ schools worldwide) uses multi-age "studios" with the "Hero's Journey" framework emphasizing character development. Specific practices include daily social-emotional check-ins, peer accountability through "relational covenants," Socratic discussions for conflict resolution, and morning mindfulness. Their "Spark Studio" (ages 3-6) focuses explicitly on developing "responsibility, kindness, empathy, honesty."

**Prenda Microschools** (1,000+ locations, 10,000+ students) structures each day around four modes: Connect, Conquer, Collaborate, Create. The Connect mode specifically designs for relationship-building and establishing culture based on core values through reading, discussion, journaling, and play. Guides work one-on-one with students on emotional self-regulation, teaching breathing exercises for managing frustration.

**KaiPod Learning** published comprehensive microschool SEL guidance recommending morning meetings, growth mindset culture, Zones of Regulation implementation, Calm Down Corners, SEL Progress Reports, and service learning integration.

**Montessori School of San Antonio** adopted Yale's RULER program in January 2023, layering it over existing practices: peace tables, grace and courtesy lessons, classroom meetings, and multi-age studios.

### Evidence-based programs suited to small settings

| Program | Ages | Format | Evidence | Microschool Fit |
|---------|------|--------|----------|-----------------|
| **Second Step Early Learning** | 4-5 | 5-7 min daily, puppets/songs | CASEL SELect | Excellent—designed for flexible, brief use |
| **Al's Pals** | 3-6 | 46 lessons with puppets | CASEL SELect | Excellent—small group interaction design |
| **Preschool PATHS** | 3-6 | 33 lessons (15-20 min) | Strong RCT evidence | Excellent—brief lessons, concrete techniques |
| **Zones of Regulation** | All ages | Color-coded framework | Evidence-based | Excellent—visual, individualized |
| **RULER** | All ages | 5-skill framework | Yale research | Good—adaptable to any setting |

**Second Step Early Learning** offers daily 5-7 minute activities with minimal preparation, using puppets and songs to engage young children. Research demonstrates improved executive function skills (attention, memory, self-control) and kindergarten readiness.

**Preschool PATHS** provides 33 brief lessons organized into four units: readiness/self-control, feelings/relationships, self-control (including the concrete "Turtle Technique"), and social problem-solving. It represents one of the most extensively evaluated early childhood SEL programs.

**Zones of Regulation** uses a color-coded system (Blue = sad/tired, Green = calm, Yellow = stressed, Red = angry) enabling children to identify their current state and select regulation strategies. Its visual concreteness makes it particularly effective for ages 4-6 and easy to reference throughout the day.

### Daily structure that embeds SEL naturally

A practical daily structure for ages 4-6 in a microschool setting:

**Morning meeting (15-20 minutes)**
- Individual greeting at door (2-3 min): child chooses greeting type
- Feelings check-in using visual aids (5-7 min): emotion cards or Zones colors
- Brief sharing or SEL story (5-7 min)
- Day preview and personal goal-setting (3-5 min)

**Throughout the day:**
- Transitions: breathing exercises, movement activities
- Work time: embedded goal-tracking, choice boards, partner work
- Snack/lunch: family-style eating practicing social conversation
- Conflict moments: Peace Corner/Table use with guide facilitation
- Closing circle: reflection, gratitude sharing, goodbye routine

**Essential physical element: Calm Down Corner.** Located in a quiet space with soft lighting, equipped with comfortable seating, fidget tools, visual breathing guides, emotion identification cards, calming bottles, and simple feelings books. Children self-select or are gently directed—never as punishment. This provides the concrete self-regulation support that ages 4-6 require.

### Multi-age groupings create natural SEL opportunities

The 4-6 age span in a single microschool creates natural peer learning dynamics documented in Montessori and Acton research:
- **Buddy systems**: Pair older children (5-6) with younger (4) for routines, read-alouds
- **Cross-age teaching**: Older children reinforce their own learning while building younger children's confidence
- **Mixed-age morning meetings**: Older children model participation; younger contribute at their level
- **Conflict resolution**: Older children practice patience; younger observe mature models

This structure mirrors family dynamics, reducing competition and increasing cooperation while allowing natural differentiation without formal tracking.

### Assessment in intimate settings relies on observation

Standardized SEL assessments designed for larger populations often feel inappropriate in microschool contexts. **Portfolio assessment** emerges as the most recommended approach:
- Weekly anecdotal notes on social interactions
- Observations of emotional regulation strategies used
- Conflict resolution examples documented
- Artwork/drawings showing emotional expression
- Goal-tracking sheets
- Self-reflection pages (for 5-6 year olds)

**Formal tools adaptable to small settings:**
- HighScope COR (Child Observation Record): observation-based scoring
- Teaching Strategies GOLD: 38 objectives, ongoing assessment
- Work Sampling System: portfolios + checklists + developmental guidelines

**For ages 4-6 self-assessment:**
- Picture-based feeling check-ins
- Thumbs up/middle/down for "How did I do?"
- Color-coding using Zones vocabulary
- Simple binary goal review: "Did I work toward my goal?"

## Methodological limitations and areas of genuine uncertainty

### What the research cannot yet tell us

**Direct microschool evidence is thin.** Most SEL research emerges from conventional classroom settings (20-30 students). While small-group instruction logically supports SEL development, formal studies specifically examining microschool outcomes remain rare. The assumption that microschool contexts amplify program effects—while plausible—is not yet empirically confirmed.

**Long-term pandemic recovery trajectories remain unknown.** Longitudinal tracking beyond 2-3 years post-pandemic is limited. Whether pandemic-era developmental deficits are permanent, partially remedial, or fully recoverable remains uncertain. The "COVID cohort" children now entering later elementary school represent an ongoing natural experiment.

**Optimal dosage thresholds are imprecise.** While research confirms that low dosage underperforms, the exact minimum effective dose remains undefined. Is 10 minutes daily equivalent to 20 minutes every other day? Does weekend interruption matter? These questions lack definitive answers.

**Explicit vs. integrated direct comparisons are rare.** Most studies evaluate single approaches rather than head-to-head comparisons. The recommendation to combine both emerges from aggregating separate literatures rather than controlled contrasts.

### Conflicts of interest in SEL research

A meaningful portion of program effectiveness research is conducted by program developers or affiliated researchers, creating potential bias. Studies conducted by independent evaluators tend to show smaller effect sizes than developer-conducted studies. When selecting programs, prioritize those with:
- What Works Clearinghouse reviews (independent federal evaluation)
- CASEL SELect designation (requires independent evidence)
- Multiple studies by unaffiliated researchers

### Methodological weaknesses in the literature

- **Cross-sectional dominance**: Most studies capture single time points, limiting causal inference
- **Proxy reporting bias**: Many studies rely on parent/teacher reports rather than direct observation of children
- **Publication bias**: Studies finding significant effects are more likely to be published
- **Sample representativeness**: Large databases aren't always nationally representative
- **Limited follow-up data**: U.S. programs showed no overall effect 6+ months post-program (Cipriano 2024), though international studies showed better sustainability

## What we know with confidence versus what remains debated

### Strong consensus exists around:
- Combining explicit instruction with integration outperforms either alone
- Teacher-delivered programs outperform external specialist delivery
- Programs meeting SAFE criteria produce stronger effects
- Foundational intrapersonal skills (self-awareness, self-regulation) should precede interpersonal skills instruction
- The pandemic negatively impacted children's social-emotional development, with youngest children most affected
- Adult educator well-being directly affects SEL delivery quality
- Low student-teacher ratios theoretically support SEL development

### Active debates and uncertainties include:
- Precise dosage requirements for different age groups
- Whether microschool/homeschool contexts produce different outcomes than classroom contexts
- Long-term sustainability of SEL gains after programs end
- Effectiveness of purely digital SEL delivery
- Whether post-pandemic cohorts require fundamentally different approaches
- Optimal balance between structured curricula and responsive, emergent SEL practices

## Conclusion: Building an evidence-informed approach for your microschool

For a private microschool serving ages 4-6, the research points toward a specific implementation strategy. **Begin with a structured, evidence-based program meeting SAFE criteria**—Second Step Early Learning (5-7 minutes daily) or Preschool PATHS (15-20 minute lessons) both carry strong evidence and fit small-group contexts. Layer this explicit instruction with Zones of Regulation as a persistent visual framework children can reference throughout the day.

**Structure the environment intentionally**: morning meeting with feelings check-in, Calm Down Corner with appropriate materials, closing circle with reflection. These routines provide the predictable scaffolding that ages 4-6 require while creating natural touchpoints for SEL practice. Leverage multi-age dynamics through buddy systems and peer mentoring, which build empathy in older children while providing models for younger ones.

**Assessment should emphasize portfolio documentation** over standardized measures: weekly anecdotal notes, observed conflict resolution episodes, emotional vocabulary growth, and goal-tracking. This approach honors individual development trajectories rather than comparing children against external norms.

**Recognize that post-pandemic cohorts may require extended timelines** for foundational skill development. Children entering your microschool in 2026 experienced critical developmental windows during unusual social circumstances. Building robust emotion vocabulary, basic turn-taking, and self-calming strategies may require more explicit attention and practice than pre-2020 cohorts needed.

The microschool structure offers genuine advantages—the relationship depth, real-time coaching, and individualized attention that research identifies as essential for this age group. The limitation is that these advantages remain theoretically supported rather than empirically demonstrated in microschool-specific studies. Your implementation becomes, in effect, applied research: a chance to document what works in the intimate context that large-scale studies cannot capture.
