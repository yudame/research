Research the relationship between diet and cardiovascular health, focusing on evidence beyond general dietary patterns. Target audience: 40-year-old men.

Key areas to investigate:
1. Saturated fat and cardiovascular outcomes: How do RCT findings compare to observational data? What explains discrepancies?
2. Ultra-processed foods: How do they affect cardiovascular risk independently of nutrient content? What mechanisms are proposed?
3. Added sugar dose-response: What is the relationship between sugar consumption levels and cardiovascular disease risk?
4. Microplastics and nanoplastics: What does emerging research reveal about their presence in cardiovascular tissue and potential effects?
5. Dietary cholesterol vs saturated fat: How important is dietary cholesterol compared to saturated fat for cardiovascular health?
6. Food matrix effects: What does evidence suggest about whole food sources vs isolated nutrients?

**Research methodology:**
- Prioritize peer-reviewed studies, meta-analyses, systematic reviews, and authoritative sources
- Distinguish between correlation and causation in findings
- Report effect sizes and practical significance, not just statistical significance
- Note the study populations and whether findings generalize to healthy middle-aged men
- Compare individual studies against meta-analyses and systematic reviews
- Identify preliminary research vs. well-replicated findings
- Note funding sources and potential conflicts of interest when relevant
- Include contradictory findings and areas of scientific uncertainty
- Cite specific studies, researchers, and sources throughout
- Provide full source URLs for all citations

**Output:** Comprehensive research report with extensive citations, sample sizes, methodological details, and source links.
