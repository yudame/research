## Research Phase

### Research Prompt

**Tool Used:** [To be specified by user - e.g., Claude, Gemini, ChatGPT, Perplexity, Grok, etc.]

**Prompt:**
```
Research market entry strategy for launching a third mobile network operator in Solomon Islands' duopoly market (Our Telekom vs Bmobile-Vodafone).

**Context:** Solomon Islands has ~740K population across 1,000+ islands (85% rural), 70% mobile phone ownership, but duopoly market with no number portability. Episode 1 established financial inclusion opportunity (80% cash economy, 75% unbanked, M-SELEN reached 350K users in 2 years). This episode examines how a NEW third operator can compete using strategic partnerships: SATSOL (fiber infrastructure + Starlink + enterprise relationships) and IumiCash (licensed mobile money + international remittance + agent network). Target audience: telecom executives, infrastructure investors, business strategists.

**Core research questions:**

1. **Market Structure:** What market share do Our Telekom and Bmobile-Vodafone hold? How do pricing, service quality, and coverage differ? What customer segments are underserved? How does Our Telekom's government ownership affect competitive dynamics? How does absence of number portability affect customer switching behavior?

2. **Regulatory Landscape:** What licenses, spectrum allocation, and approvals are required under TCSI (Telecommunications Commission Solomon Islands) framework? What infrastructure sharing opportunities exist? What pricing regulations apply? What timelines should a new entrant expect? Is TCSI pro-competition or protectionist?

3. **Market Entry Lessons:** How did Digicel achieve 2,400% subscription growth in PNG post-2007 entry? What strategies worked/failed in Digicel's Fiji, Samoa, Vanuatu entries? What patterns exist in successful Pacific telecom disruptions? What customer pain points create entry opportunities?

4. **Strategic Positioning:** How do you position a third player in a duopoly? What positioning strategies (price, quality, coverage, segments) work for market entry? How do SATSOL infrastructure capabilities and IumiCash payment integration create differentiation from day one that incumbents can't easily match?

**Research methodology:**
- Prioritize official sources: TCSI reports, regulatory filings, market statistics, operator disclosures
- Cite specific data: market share percentages, subscriber counts, coverage statistics, pricing comparisons
- Distinguish between documented facts and industry speculation
- Compare Solomon Islands to other Pacific island nations (PNG, Fiji, Samoa, Vanuatu)
- Analyze case studies with specific outcomes: growth rates, market share changes, timeline metrics
- Report effect sizes and practical significance, not just general observations
- Include contradictory findings and areas of uncertainty
- Note funding sources and potential conflicts of interest when relevant
- Provide actionable strategic insights, not generic observations

**Output:** Comprehensive research report addressing all four question areas with specific data points, regulatory citations, case study metrics, competitive analysis, strategic recommendations, and full source links throughout.
```

**Date:** 2025-12-05

Core principles:
• Spell out acronyms first: "Telecommunications Commission Solomon Islands, or TCSI" - then use acronym
• Define technical terms immediately in plain language before building on them
• Use concrete examples ONLY from source material - never fabricate
• Highlight findings that reveal strategic lessons or challenge assumptions
• Extract frameworks and connect to practical implications
• Maintain scientific rigor: distinguish correlation from causation, note effect sizes and uncertainties

Emphasis areas:
• Spell-first for acronyms, definition-first for technical terms
• Evidence-based analysis: cite studies, report effect sizes, note sample sizes
• Include human elements when they exist: decisions made, reasoning, outcomes
• Use conversational check-ins: "Let me define that term..." or "To be clear..."
• Translate findings to practical meaning and broader patterns

Highlight insights worth examining:
• Counter-intuitive findings that reveal strategic lessons
• Failures that illustrate specific mistakes or systemic issues
• Unexpected outcomes that challenge assumptions
• Make numbers meaningful through context and comparisons

Avoid:
• Undefined acronyms and jargon
• Academic language when simpler words work
• Introducing 3+ new technical terms in one sentence
• Fabricated examples or over-hedging that obscures findings
• Dry explanations when human stories exist in research
• Repeatedly restating context

Target: Intelligent listeners wanting deep understanding and practical insights. Appreciate technical depth but need terms defined.

Tone: Intellectually rigorous but accessible - "conversational expert explaining to a bright student"

When presenting stories:
• Include decision-making context: "Digicel invested $160M in 120 towers, which led to Y" not "The market experienced growth"
• Provide specific details: "In 2007, Digicel entered PNG with aggressive pricing..." not "Digicel had market impact"
• Use precise numbers for context: "$6.96 per GB ranks 14th most expensive globally" not "data is expensive"
• Show scale through comparisons: "77 times more expensive than Fiji" - comparison creates impact
• Connect to lessons: Explain what the outcome reveals about systems, incentives, or strategy

When presenting research: Focus on what numbers mean, use comparisons ("77x more than Fiji"), translate statistics to implications.

Closing: Summarize 2-3 key takeaways, close with "Find full research and sources at research dot yuda dot me - that's Y-U-D-A dot M-E"
