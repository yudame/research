# Research Prompt: Episode 2 - Breaking the Duopoly

## Episode Context

**Series:** Solomon Islands Telecom Launch Series (Episode 2 of 6)

**Episode Title:** Breaking the Duopoly - Market Entry Strategy

**Previous Episode Context:** Episode 1 established the financial inclusion opportunity in Solomon Islands—80% cash economy, 75% unbanked population, but 70% mobile phone ownership. M-SELEN (Our Telekom's mobile money) reached 350,000 users in 2 years, proving massive latent demand for financial services. This episode explores how a NEW third mobile network operator can enter this duopoly market with strategic partnerships from SATSOL (infrastructure) and IumiCash (mobile money).

**Target Audience:** Telecommunications executives, infrastructure investors, business strategists, and policy makers interested in competitive market entry in challenging geographies.

---

## Research Objectives

Analyze the competitive landscape, regulatory framework, and strategic positioning options for launching a third mobile network operator in the Solomon Islands duopoly market (Our Telekom vs Bmobile-Vodafone). Understand what lessons Pacific market disruptions offer, how regulatory barriers and opportunities shape entry strategy, and how partnership advantages (SATSOL infrastructure + IumiCash payments) create differentiation from day one.

---

## Core Research Questions

### Market Structure and Competition

1. **How have duopoly markets (Our Telekom vs Bmobile-Vodafone) shaped the Solomon Islands telecom landscape?**
   - What market share does each operator hold (mobile subscriptions, revenue, coverage)?
   - How do pricing, service quality, and coverage differ between the two incumbents?
   - What customer segments does each operator serve, and are there underserved segments?
   - How has the duopoly affected innovation, pricing, and service quality over time?

2. **What role does government ownership of Our Telekom play in competitive dynamics?**
   - How does Our Telekom's government ownership affect regulatory treatment, spectrum allocation, or infrastructure access?
   - What advantages or constraints does government ownership create for Our Telekom?
   - How have other Pacific nations handled government-owned incumbent operators when new entrants arrive?

3. **How does absence of number portability affect customer switching behavior and market entry strategy?**
   - What evidence exists on customer switching rates in markets without number portability?
   - How do customers behave when they can't port their numbers (dual SIM usage, complete switching, staying with incumbent)?
   - What implications does this have for customer acquisition strategy for a new entrant?

### Regulatory Landscape

4. **What regulatory barriers and opportunities exist for new market entrants under TCSI (Telecommunications Commission Solomon Islands) framework?**
   - What licenses are required to operate as a mobile network operator?
   - What spectrum allocation process exists, and what frequencies are available for new entrants?
   - Are there infrastructure sharing requirements or opportunities (tower access, backhaul)?
   - What pricing regulations exist (price controls, transparency requirements, tariff approvals)?
   - What consumer protection regulations apply (service quality standards, complaint resolution)?
   - What timelines and approval processes should a new entrant expect?

5. **How does TCSI's regulatory approach compare to other Pacific island nations?**
   - Is TCSI pro-competition or protectionist toward incumbents?
   - What regulatory reforms have occurred recently, and what direction is policy moving?

### Market Entry Lessons

6. **What lessons can be learned from successful (and failed) market disruptions in Pacific nations?**
   - **Digicel in PNG:** How did they achieve 2,400% subscription growth post-2007 entry? What strategies worked?
   - **Digicel in Fiji, Samoa, Vanuatu:** What market entry approaches succeeded or failed across different Pacific markets?
   - What common patterns exist in successful Pacific telecom market disruptions?
   - What mistakes did unsuccessful or slow-growth entrants make?

7. **What customer pain points exist with incumbent providers that create entry opportunities?**
   - What complaints or frustrations do customers express about Our Telekom and Bmobile?
   - Where do incumbents underperform (coverage gaps, pricing, customer service, data quality, billing transparency)?
   - What unmet needs exist (rural connectivity, affordable data, reliable service, mobile money integration)?

### Strategic Positioning

8. **How do you position a third player in a duopoly market?**
   - Should positioning emphasize price competition, service quality, geographic coverage, or customer segmentation?
   - What successful positioning strategies exist from other duopoly market entries globally?
   - How does "challenger brand" messaging resonate in Pacific island cultures?

9. **How do SATSOL infrastructure capabilities and IumiCash payment integration create differentiation from day one?**
   - What competitive advantages does partnering with an established infrastructure provider (SATSOL) create vs. building from scratch?
   - How does launching with integrated mobile money (IumiCash) create differentiation vs. incumbents?
   - What value propositions emerge from bundled connectivity + financial services that incumbents can't easily match?
   - How does IumiCash's international remittance capability (MTS license) create unique customer value?

## Research Methodology

### Prioritize Evidence-Based Analysis
- Use official sources: TCSI reports, regulatory filings, market statistics, operator disclosures
- Cite specific data: market share percentages, subscriber counts, coverage statistics, pricing comparisons
- Distinguish between documented facts and industry speculation

### Comparative Analysis
- Compare Solomon Islands market structure to other Pacific island nations (PNG, Fiji, Samoa, Vanuatu)
- Analyze lessons from successful market entries (Digicel's Pacific expansion, other regional examples)
- Identify what worked vs. what failed in similar competitive contexts

### Regulatory Deep Dive
- Review TCSI regulatory framework, licensing requirements, and recent policy decisions
- Identify barriers to entry (spectrum availability, infrastructure access, capital requirements)
- Identify opportunities (pro-competition policies, infrastructure sharing mandates, regulatory support for new entrants)

### Customer Perspective
- What do Solomon Islanders actually experience with current operators?
- Where are the pain points that create switching motivation?
- What customer segments are underserved or dissatisfied?

### Partnership Advantage Analysis
- How does SATSOL partnership change market entry economics and timeline?
- How does IumiCash integration create defensible differentiation?
- What synergies exist between infrastructure access and payment services?

---

## Key Considerations

### Geographic and Cultural Context
- Solomon Islands: ~740,000 population across 1,000+ islands, 85% rural
- Pijin as dominant language alongside English
- Cash culture, limited banking access, high remittance dependence
- Government ownership of Our Telekom creates unique competitive dynamics

### Competitive Reality
- Duopoly markets are notoriously hard to disrupt without differentiation
- No number portability means customer acquisition must overcome significant friction
- Incumbents have entrenched distribution, brand recognition, and customer relationships

### Partnership Differentiation
- SATSOL: fiber infrastructure + Starlink access + enterprise relationships
- IumiCash: licensed mobile money + remittance capability + agent network
- Combined: integrated connectivity + fintech value proposition
