Solomon Islands Telecom Launch

A 6-part series exploring strategic partnership-enabled market entry in Pacific telecommunications

Episodes

Ep 1 Financial Infrastructure 30:25 Report Transcript
The financial ecosystem and payment infrastructure landscape in the Solomon Islands.
More details
Explores the 80% cash economy, M-SELEN's 350,000-user explosion in 2 years, banking infrastructure limitations (15 branches, 59 ATMs, 72%+ concentrated in Honiara), mobile money competitors, utility payments as gateway use case, remittance flows, CBSI regulatory framework, and the Bokolo Cash CBDC pilot. Establishes the financial inclusion opportunity (75% unbanked, 70% have mobile phones) and why telecom + payments integration matters for launching a new provider with mobile money from day one.
Ep 2 Breaking the Duopoly 37:31 Report Transcript
Market entry strategy and competitive positioning for a third mobile network operator.
More details
Analyzes how the Our Telekom vs Bmobile-Vodafone duopoly shaped the market, regulatory barriers and opportunities under TCSI framework, lessons from Digicel's 2,400% subscription growth in PNG, how absence of number portability affects switching behavior, customer pain points with incumbents, positioning strategy (price vs service quality vs coverage), government ownership dynamics of Our Telekom, and how SATSOL infrastructure + IumiCash integration create differentiation from launch.
Ep 3 Infrastructure Advantage 35:25 Report Transcript
Leveraging SATSOL's fiber/Starlink infrastructure to solve the 1,000-island challenge.
More details
Examines infrastructure investment typically required for archipelago networks, how partnerships change economics vs build-from-scratch, Starlink satellite backhaul vs traditional submarine cable solutions, SATSOL's fiber network role in urban coverage, tower deployment strategy across 85% rural population with SINBIP integration, technical architecture balancing fiber/Starlink/cellular, network resilience in disaster-prone regions, spectrum and tower siting regulatory requirements, and SATSOL's ISO certifications supporting reliable backhaul.
Ep 4 Mobile Money Integration 39:54 Report Transcript
Launching with integrated mobile money capabilities that incumbents took years to build.
More details
Analyzes M-SELEN's 350,000-user achievement in two years and demand signals, competitive advantages of integrated mobile money from launch, IumiCash's Money Transfer Service license enabling international remittances, CBSI regulatory framework for telecom-affiliated payments, business relationship structuring (revenue sharing, customer data, agent networks), use cases driving adoption, pricing for bundled services, agent network infrastructure vs M-SELEN's 3,000+ agents, competing against Our Telekom's vertical integration, and smartphone penetration implications (IumiCash smartphone-only vs M-SELEN USSD).
Ep 5 Launch Execution 32:54 Report Transcript
End-to-end go-to-market execution from spectrum allocation to first customer activation.
More details
Covers TCSI licenses and spectrum allocations required for third MNO, initial network coverage strategy (which islands first and why), typical timeline from approval to commercial launch in Pacific markets, SIM card distribution across dispersed islands, pricing strategy to break duopoly (promotional/family/data/bundled), early adopter acquisition without number portability, customer segment targeting, handset strategy balancing 4G capability and affordability, leveraging SATSOL's enterprise relationships and IumiCash's merchant network, customer support infrastructure across islands, and brand positioning communication.
Ep 6 The Smartphone Frontier 36:49 Report Transcript
Serving first-time smartphone users with device provisioning strategies as market expansion tool.
More details
Explores the 59-percentage-point gap between network coverage (86%) and mobile internet usage (27%) across the Pacific. Examines five interlocking barriers: device affordability (phones cost 30% of monthly income for poorest segments), infrastructure quality (data costs 38% of income), digital literacy (42% lack basic skills), content/language availability, and gender inequality (women 25% less likely to own smartphones). Analyzes device financing models (PAYGO, BNPL), China's subsidy experiment results, the lock-in problem with missing number portability, mobile money-smartphone interdependence, Starlink's connectivity transformation, and Vanuatu's "Play or Pay" policy contrast showing how coordinated regulation can drive inclusion beyond market forces alone.
