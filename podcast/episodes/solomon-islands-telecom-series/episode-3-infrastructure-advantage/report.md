## Executive Summary

Launching a mobile network across Solomon Islands' 1,000+ islands traditionally requires **$50-150 million in CAPEX** and **3-5 years** of deployment time. Infrastructure partnerships fundamentally transform this equation, reducing initial capital requirements by **50-80%** and compressing deployment timelines from years to months.

Three converging developments have created a window for viable market entry that didn't exist even five years ago:

1. **SINBIP Infrastructure Sharing**: 161 government-funded tower sites accessible to all operators by 2026, eliminating the largest CAPEX burden
2. **Starlink Satellite Backhaul**: LEO satellite providing 100-350 Mbps with 25-60ms latency, deployable in days versus years for traditional backhaul
3. **SATSOL Partnership Model**: Existing fiber network, Starlink exclusivity, and ISO certifications enabling turnkey connectivity solutions

For a partnership-enabled entrant, total initial CAPEX could fall to **$15-30 million**—an order of magnitude below traditional greenfield deployments—while achieving comparable coverage and superior resilience.

## The Capital Equation Shifts Dramatically

### Traditional Greenfield Deployment Economics

Building a mobile network from scratch across Pacific islands typically breaks down as follows for a 100-500 site deployment:

| Cost Category | Build-from-Scratch | Partnership Model | Savings |
|--------------|-------------------|------------------|---------|
| Tower infrastructure (100 sites) | $13.5-33.7M | $4-12M (co-location) | 50-70% |
| Backhaul network | $5-30M | Lease-based OPEX | 80-90% |
| Spectrum licensing | $1-20M | Same | 0% |
| Core network | $5-30M | Cloud-native option | 30-50% |
| Power systems | $2-15M | Shared sites | 40-60% |
| **Total Estimated** | **$26-129M** | **$10-50M** | **60-70%** |

**Per-site costs** in archipelago deployments reach **$135,000-$337,000**—a 35% premium over global averages—driven by helicopter deployments, satellite terminals at every site, and hybrid solar-diesel power systems.

### The Digicel Benchmark

Digicel invested over **$850 million** building Papua New Guinea's mobile network from scratch over 15 years. A partnership-based entrant could achieve comparable coverage for a fraction of that investment by leveraging existing infrastructure rather than building everything anew.

### Timeline Compression

Traditional tower construction in remote Pacific sites takes **6-18 months per site**. Co-location on existing infrastructure reduces this to **days or weeks**. For backhaul, submarine cable deployment requires **3-5 years** from planning to operation. Starlink deployment: **days to weeks**.

This timeline compression translates directly to revenue acceleration—operators can begin service delivery and cash flow generation years earlier under partnership models.

## SINBIP: 161 Ready-to-Use Tower Sites by 2026

The **Solomon Islands National Broadband Infrastructure Project** represents the single most transformational infrastructure development for mobile operators. Funded through a **CNY 448.9 million (~$66-100 million)** concessional loan from Exim Bank of China at 1.0% interest over 20 years, SINBIP will deliver 161 3G/4G-capable tower sites with microwave and VSAT backhaul connections.

### Current Deployment Status (September 2024)

- **Phase 1**: 14 sites operational (13 Guadalcanal + 1 Russell Islands VSAT site)
- **Phase 2**: Choiseul and Western Provinces deployment commenced
- **Phase 3**: Malaita, Makira, Temotu, Renbel targeted for Q1 2025

### The Infrastructure Sharing Model

**Solomon Tower Limited (STL)**, a new state-owned enterprise, owns and manages this infrastructure. The government explicitly designed a **public-private partnership model** enabling infrastructure sharing with all operators—Solomon Telekom, bmobile, ISPs, and new entrants.

STL will negotiate commercial lease terms with operators; revenues service the Exim Bank loan. This model converts prohibitive CAPEX into predictable OPEX, allowing operators to **save up to 40% of capital expenditures** by sharing passive infrastructure.

Tower specifications include:
- Solar power systems at most sites (reducing diesel dependency)
- 1.8-meter VSAT dishes for baseline connectivity
- 3G/4G-capable equipment mounting
- Cyclone-hardened construction for resilience

### Timeline Reality Check

While SINBIP aims for 161 towers by 2026, actual deployment faces challenges:

**Per-tower timeline (realistic):**
- Land acquisition: 2-4 months (longest delay factor)
- Permitting: 6-8 months (TCSI process)
- Tower construction: 6-8 weeks
- Backhaul deployment: 4-12 weeks (parallel with tower)
- RAN installation: 2-3 weeks
- **Total: 6-12 months per site**

**Critical bottleneck**: Land disputes are THE blocker, not technology. Approximately **87% of Solomon Islands land is customary land**, managed by local indigenous groups rather than formal government title systems. Tower siting requires community consent through direct negotiation with landowners—a process that can extend from months to years.

**Phase 1 delays**: Targeted 48 towers by November 2023, delivered 13 by mid-2025 (12-18 month delay) due to land disputes affecting 30-40% of sites and permitting bottlenecks.

## Starlink Transforms Backhaul Economics

### The Traditional False Binary

The historical choice between submarine cables and satellite backhaul was fundamentally compromised:

- **Submarine cables**: Massive bandwidth at low per-gigabyte costs but requiring **$6,000-50,000 per kilometer** in capital investment and **3-5 years** from planning to operation
- **Geostationary satellites**: Coverage anywhere but with 500-600ms latency that degraded voice quality and made modern data services unusable

LEO satellite constellations, particularly Starlink, have created a third option that changes the calculus for island networks.

### KDDI Japan: The Proof Point

**1,200 mobile towers** now use Starlink backhaul across Japan's 6,000+ islands and 16,000+ mountains. KDDI's extensive evaluation confirmed that Starlink "met network technical guidelines for latency, jitter, and uplink/downlink bandwidth" with "customer experience comparable to optical fiber."

The service launched commercially in December 2022 on Hatsushima island, validating Starlink as viable cellular backhaul infrastructure in operational deployment at scale.

### Technical Specifications Comparison

| Factor | Starlink (LEO) | Submarine Cable | GEO Satellite | Microwave |
|--------|---------------|----------------|---------------|-----------|
| **Latency** | 25-60ms (100+ms islands) | 5-10ms per 1,000km | 500-600ms | Near-zero |
| **Bandwidth per site** | 100-350 Mbps | Terabits (shared) | 50-200 Mbps | 1-20 Gbps |
| **CAPEX per site** | $2,500-10,000 | Millions (amortized) | $15,000-50,000 | $5,000-50,000 |
| **Monthly OPEX** | $300-500 | IRU/lease fees | $2,000-10,000 | Spectrum + maintenance |
| **Deployment time** | Days-weeks | 3-5 years | Weeks-months | Weeks-months |
| **Physical vulnerability** | None | High (anchor/fishing) | None | Low |

### Starlink Pricing Structure

Enterprise backhaul applications:
- Terminal costs: **$2,500-10,000** (depending on configuration)
- Monthly service fees: **$300-500** for business plans
- 2025 maritime/global pricing: $150/month terminal access fee + **$1-2/GB** for data consumption

This creates predictable unit economics for network planning—far more manageable than the massive upfront capital required for submarine cable landing or multi-year microwave network construction.

### Monthly Backhaul Cost Comparison

For a **10 Mbps tower link** (typical remote island cell):

| Technology | CAPEX | Monthly OPEX | 5-Year TCO | $/Mbps/month |
|------------|-------|--------------|------------|--------------|
| **Starlink** | $1.5K | $500-800 | $31K-50K | $50-80 |
| **Kacific-1** | $8K-11K | $750-950 | $63.5K | $150-190 |
| **Microwave** | $5K-8K | $0 | $5K-8K | $0-50 |
| **Submarine (capital share)** | $2M-5M | $50-100 | $12M+ | $5-20 |

**Key insight**: Microwave wins on TCO if line-of-sight available (<20 km). Beyond that, **Starlink is cheapest** for immediate deployment.

### The Critical Caveat

Starlink's official documentation explicitly states that "customers in certain remote locations will experience higher latency (e.g., Oceans, Islands, Antarctica)." Real-world latency of **100ms or more** for Pacific island locations must be factored into core network architecture decisions.

However, this still represents a **5x improvement** over GEO satellite latency and remains adequate for 4G/5G mobile services when edge User Plane Functions (UPF) process local traffic without backhauling to distant cloud cores.

## Hybrid Architecture: The Optimal Strategy

The optimal backhaul strategy for archipelago networks combines multiple technologies based on geography and traffic density.

### Recommended Architecture Pattern

- **Main trunk**: Submarine cable (Coral Sea Cable System - 4,700km, 40 Tbps capacity) to Honiara gateway
- **Inter-island distribution**: Microwave links for islands within 50km with line-of-sight (Digicel demonstrated 189km microwave link in Tonga)
- **Remote islands**: Starlink terminals for sites beyond microwave range
- **Disaster redundancy**: Satellite backup for all fiber-connected sites

### Solomon Islands Existing Infrastructure

The **Coral Sea Cable System** reduced wholesale capacity costs by up to **80%** compared to pre-cable satellite-only connectivity. The **Solomon Islands Domestic Network (SIDN)** extends 730km of submarine cable to connect Honiara with Auki, Noro, and Taro.

Our Telekom currently uses this combination of cable and satellite for remote sites, demonstrating the hybrid model in practice.

### Partnership Advantage

For a new entrant partnering with SATSOL (which already has fiber access and Starlink capability), the backhaul problem essentially disappears from the capital stack. Instead of $20-30 million in backhaul infrastructure investment, the operator pays predictable monthly fees per site while maintaining geographic flexibility to add coverage incrementally.

**Hybrid approach TCO impact**: This multi-technology strategy reduces total 5-year backhaul TCO by **25-35%** versus single-technology approach while dramatically improving resilience.

## Climate Resilience: Satellite's Killer Application

### The Tonga Volcanic Eruption Case Study

The January 2022 Tonga volcanic eruption and tsunami demonstrated the fragility of submarine cable infrastructure in catastrophic events. The 827km cable connecting Tonga to Fiji wasn't merely cut—an **80km section was shredded into multiple pieces**, moved 5km by undersea blast forces, and buried under sediment.

**Tonga communication impact:**
- International connectivity: **38-day complete outage**
- Domestic cable restoration: **18 months**
- Cable repair ship transit: 8+ days from Port Moresby (4,200km away)
- Daily ship costs: **$35,000-50,000**
- Total damage estimate: **$90.4 million** (World Bank GRADE assessment)
- Economy-wide loss: **~$100 million**

### Pacific Cyclone Impacts

| Event | Total Damage | Telecom Impact |
|-------|-------------|----------------|
| Cyclone Winston (Fiji 2016) | $1.4 billion | 80% lost power, island communications severed |
| Cyclone Pam (Vanuatu 2015) | $449 million | One tower operational in Port Vila post-cyclone |
| Cyclone Harold (2020) | Multi-country | Communications destroyed across 7+ islands |
| Vanuatu volcanic eruption (April 2025) | Ongoing | 72-hour near-total outage; Starlink at resorts saved situation |

### The 4:1 Return on Resilience Investment

**World Bank analysis** confirms that investing in resilient telecommunications infrastructure delivers a **4:1 return**—$4 in avoided losses and accelerated recovery for every $1 invested in hardening.

For island networks, this translates to:
- Cyclone-rated tower designs (wind ratings of 200-320+ km/h)
- Reinforced equipment shelters
- Backup power systems with **48-72 hours of autonomy** for critical sites
- Satellite backup backhaul for cable-connected sites

### Satellite Inherent Resilience

Starlink deployed over **800 emergency kits** across 120+ missions following Florida hurricanes, California wildfires, and Maui disasters. With no ground-based single points of failure beyond the terminal itself, Starlink service can continue operating through events that sever cables or destroy towers—provided the terminal and power system survive.

**Policy implication**: Development banks (World Bank, ADB) now require disaster-resilient architecture in funded projects. Insurance/business continuity premiums justify satellite investment as non-negotiable infrastructure layer.

## Low-Band Spectrum: The Coverage Economics Foundation

Network architecture decisions fundamentally shape deployment economics across dispersed island populations. Solomon Islands' 85% rural population living across 1,000+ islands requires coverage-focused spectrum strategy rather than capacity-first approaches suited to dense urban markets.

### 700 MHz: The Coverage Multiplier

A single **700 MHz cell site** provides **5-10km coverage radius** compared to 2-3km for 1800 MHz—reducing required site counts by approximately **50%** and coverage costs by **70%** versus 2100 MHz deployments.

**Coverage comparison by frequency:**

| Frequency | Typical Rural Radius | Sites for Equivalent Coverage | Relative Cost |
|-----------|---------------------|------------------------------|---------------|
| 700 MHz | 5-10 km | 1x baseline | 1.0x |
| 1800 MHz | 2-3 km | 2-3x | 2.0x |
| 2100 MHz | 2-3 km | 2-3x | 2.3x |
| 2600 MHz | 1 km | 4-5x | 3.5x |

### Recommended Spectrum Strategy

**For archipelago deployment**: Prioritize 700 MHz as the coverage layer across all sites, with 1800 MHz capacity supplements only in population centers like Honiara. This approach minimizes site count—the primary cost driver—while providing adequate capacity for rural population densities.

The APT700 band harmonization across Asia-Pacific provides equipment ecosystem scale benefits, reducing equipment costs through standardization.

## Open RAN and Cloud-Native Architecture

### Open RAN Cost Advantages

**Open RAN deployments** deliver **25-30% TCO reduction** compared to traditional integrated RAN solutions, with particular benefits for cost-constrained island deployments.

Benefits:
- Multi-vendor flexibility reduces supply chain risk
- Lower-cost radio units from emerging vendors (NuRAN, Parallel Wireless)
- Suitable for minimum-viable rural site configurations

### Rural Site Specifications

Cost-optimized deployment for remote islands:

| Component | Specification | Power Draw |
|-----------|--------------|------------|
| Radio unit (700 MHz, single-sector) | 150-500W | - |
| Baseband processing | Centralized or local | 100-300W |
| Satellite terminal (Starlink) | Standard business | 100-200W |
| Total site power | 350-1,000W | - |
| Solar array sizing | 4-8 kW | - |
| Battery capacity (3-day autonomy) | 25-75 kWh | - |
| **Estimated CAPEX** | **$25,000-50,000** (excluding tower) | - |

### Cloud-Native Core Benefits

Adopting cloud-native core architecture offers substantial benefits in archipelagic environments:

- Decoupling software functions from underlying hardware
- Virtualized roaming gateways minimize remote site complexity
- Central management of distributed network
- Ideal for managing high-latency satellite backhaul links
- CAPEX advantages (OPEX model versus upfront investment)

**Optimal approach**: Deploy edge User Plane Functions (UPF) at regional hubs, processing local traffic without backhauling to distant cloud cores. This hybrid architecture—cloud-native control plane with distributed user plane—balances cost efficiency with latency management for satellite backhaul.

## Regulatory Framework: Low Barriers, Strategic Gaps

### TCSI Licensing and Spectrum

Solomon Islands' telecommunications regulatory environment presents surprisingly low barriers to entry:

**Licensing pathway:**
- **Class Licence**: Standard form application, 45-day processing if criteria satisfied
- **Individual Licence**: Competitive selection process when number restricted
- **Spectrum assignment**: Administrative allocation (not auction) with **no ongoing fees**

The Telecommunications Commission Solomon Islands (TCSI) explicitly encourages competition by "maintaining one form of service licence which authorises all manner of telecommunications services, and it does not charge operators for the use of radio-spectrum."

This zero-fee spectrum allocation dramatically reduces ongoing operational costs compared to markets where spectrum fees represent significant annual OPEX.

### Current Spectrum Allocations

- **Our Telekom**: 700 MHz (Band 28), 850 MHz, 900 MHz, 1800 MHz (Band 3), 2100 MHz
- **BMobile**: 900 MHz, 1800 MHz, 2100 MHz (has not deployed 4G)
- **Starlink**: Licensed in 2024 after regulatory review

### Land Access: The Primary Challenge

Tower siting requires community consent through direct negotiation with landowners. Environmental approval under the Environment Regulations 2008 requires Environmental Impact Statements for significant developments.

**What's missing from regulatory framework:**
- **No mandatory infrastructure-sharing mandate** (unlike Thailand, Bangladesh) - currently voluntary commercial terms
- **Spectrum auction for 5G not yet scheduled** (2026-2027 expected)
- **Landing agreements for new cables** still in negotiation (government-led)

**Recommendation**: Frame TCSI as "supportive but incomplete"; infrastructure-sharing regulations expected 2026-2027.

## Conclusion: Partnership-First Strategy Unlocks Archipelago Markets

The infrastructure economics for launching mobile networks across archipelagos have fundamentally shifted. Traditional approaches requiring $100+ million in capital investment and multi-year deployment timelines face a new alternative: partnership-based models that leverage existing fiber, satellite, and tower infrastructure to achieve equivalent coverage for **$15-30 million** in initial capital and deployment timelines measured in months rather than years.

**The technical architecture for this approach combines:**

- **Starlink satellite backhaul** (100-350 Mbps, days to deploy) for remote sites
- **Fiber connectivity** (via partnerships accessing Coral Sea Cable) for main population centers
- **Low-band 700 MHz spectrum** (reduces required site counts by 50%)
- **Open RAN and cloud-native core networks** (further compress both capital and operating costs)

**For Solomon Islands specifically**, the convergence of:

1. SINBIP's 161 government-funded tower sites
2. TCSI's zero-fee spectrum licensing
3. Infrastructure partnerships with established providers (SATSOL)

...creates a window for market entry that didn't exist even five years ago.

The regulatory framework presents no structural barriers; customary land complexity for tower siting represents the primary operational challenge—but SINBIP addresses this by handling land negotiations for government sites.

**Climate resilience** provides strategic justification for satellite-first architecture beyond pure economics. When submarine cables take 38 days to 18 months to repair after catastrophic events, satellite backhaul transitions from cost-optimization strategy to business continuity requirement.

Infrastructure investors, development finance institutions, and telecom operators evaluating Pacific island opportunities should recognize that the partnership model—accessing existing fiber, Starlink backhaul, and shared tower infrastructure—transforms archipelago deployments from marginal investment cases into commercially viable opportunities with manageable risk profiles.
