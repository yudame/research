## VERIFIED KEY FINDINGS

### 1. Regulatory Licensing Process

**CONFIRMED ACROSS ALL SOURCES:**
- **Regulator:** Telecommunications Commission of Solomon Islands (TCSI) - independent statutory authority under Telecommunications Act 2009 (P, C, Ge, Cl)
- **Legal requirement:** No entity may provide telecommunications services without appropriate authorization (P, C, Ge)
- **License types:** Two primary categories exist:
  - **Class License:** Standard applications via prescribed forms, 45-day effective timeline (P, C, Cl)
  - **Individual License:** Customized for mobile operators, competitive selection/"beauty contest" process (P, Ge, Cl)

**CROSS-VALIDATION NOTES:**
- Timeline variance: Perplexity states "45 days for initial response" while ChatGPT/Claude indicate "45 days to registration effective" for class licenses. **Reconciliation:** P refers to initial TCSI response obligation; C/Cl refer to full registration timeline for class licenses. Individual licenses require longer competitive evaluation with no fixed timeline.
- Historical precedent confirmed (Ge, Cl): Bmobile received license in 2010 through competitive tender emphasizing coverage commitments over financial bids
- **SINGLE-SOURCE CLAIM (Cl):** Our Telekom's 15-year exclusivity license blocked Digicel entry 2006-2007 via High Court injunction, expired ~2018. *Not verified by other sources - treat cautiously.*

**APPLICATION REQUIREMENTS (P - most detailed):**
- Organizational details: company registration, trading name, business license
- Management composition: directors' backgrounds, residency, experience
- Technical capabilities: network design, technology platforms, deployment methodology
- **Financial qualifications:** 5-year business plan, debt/equity structure, pro-forma financials
  - **IMPORTANT:** No minimum capital threshold specified in Act (P, Ge confirmed)
  - Assessment based on competitive evaluation of demonstrated resources (P)
- Marketing strategy: customer acquisition, revenue projections
- Quality of service commitments with supporting documentation
- Network coverage plans: geographic rollout phases, timeline milestones

**REGULATORY FEES (P - most specific):**
- Annual license fees: up to 2% of gross revenues to TCSI
- Supplementary fees: if Telecommunications Commission Special Fund operates at deficit
- Universal Access Fund levy: up to 2% of gross revenues for rural service support
- Application/administrative fees: non-refundable processing charges (schedules published by TCSI)
- **Definition:** "Gross revenues" = revenues from telecom services after deducting amounts paid to other providers at arms' length, BEFORE operational costs

### 2. Spectrum Allocation

**AVAILABLE FREQUENCY BANDS (P - most comprehensive technical detail):**

| Frequency Band | Technology | Current Status | Cross-Validation |
|---|---|---|---|
| 700 MHz | 4G LTE | Allocated (3 × 15 MHz), not currently utilized | P primary; Cl confirms "available in higher bands" |
| 850 MHz (Band 5) | UMTS/4G LTE | Assigned, requires review | P only |
| 900 MHz (E-GSM) | GSM/UMTS/4G LTE | Fully allocated and utilized (high) | P, Cl confirms incumbents hold |
| 1800 MHz (DCS) | GSM/UMTS/4G LTE | Assigned and utilized, capacity available | P, Cl confirms "significant capacity" |
| 2100 MHz (Band 1) | UMTS/4G LTE | Assigned, not utilized, capacity available | P, Cl |
| 2300 MHz | 4G LTE | Assigned, not utilized, capacity available | P, Cl |
| 2500 MHz | 4G LTE | Assigned, not utilized, capacity available | P, Cl |

**Source:** National Radio Spectrum Plan (consultation draft December 2015) - P citation [5]

**STRATEGIC SPECTRUM ASSESSMENT (synthesized across sources):**
- **Low-band advantage (Cl emphasis):** 700 MHz critical for cost-effective rural coverage in archipelago setting
- **Higher band reality (Cl):** 1800/2100/2300/2500 MHz adequate for urban 4G/5G but require denser tower networks for equivalent rural coverage
- **Competitive barrier:** Incumbents (Our Telekom, Bmobile) hold prime 900/1800 MHz bands (P, Cl)

**SPECTRUM ALLOCATION PROCESS (P, C, Ge consensus):**
- Authority: TCSI under Part 7 of Telecommunications Act 2009
- Coordination: Spectrum license applications coordinated with service license applications
- Compliance: Must follow published National Spectrum Plan
- Non-discriminatory allocation: Open, non-discriminatory basis per regulatory standards
- **Separate requirement (C emphasis):** Service licenses do NOT confer spectrum rights; separate application mandatory

**SPECTRUM FEES (P):**
- Annual fees set by TCSI regulation (authority under Act)
- Revenue directed to Universal Access Special Fund for rural service subsidies
- No separate spectrum fee if bundled within service license
- Conditions may include vacation obligations, non-use remedies, compensation mechanisms

**REGIONAL PRECEDENT - VANUATU SPECTRUM SET-ASIDE (Ge detailed, Cl confirmed):**
- Vanuatu TRBR considered reserving 2 × 15 MHz in 700 MHz band specifically for "new carrier" (not incumbents) in 2016 award
- Outcome: Successfully allocated 700 MHz and AWS-1 to all three operators (CCT, Digicel, Flow)
- **Strategic application:** New SI entrant should advocate for similar "new entrant set-aside" policy for low-band spectrum to ensure competitive parity (Ge recommendation)

### 3. Network Infrastructure Requirements

**INFRASTRUCTURE INVENTORY (P - 2014 baseline data):**
- 125 GSM base stations (2G)
- 18 UMTS nodes (3G, 2100 MHz)
- 19 UMTS nodes (3G, 850 MHz)
- Connectivity: microwave links and satellite backhaul
- International gateway and earth stations in Honiara
- **Population coverage:** 83% with mobile network access (concentrated in urban/provincial capitals), 80% of population in rural villages

**CAPITAL INFRASTRUCTURE COSTS (P - most specific benchmarks):**
- **Per-site CAPEX (tower + equipment):** AUD $1-3 million for fully designed/built/commissioned towers in regional areas
  - Breakdown: Power and backhaul ~50%, RAN equipment ~30%, tower structure 20-25%
- **Backhaul costs:** 30-50% of total infrastructure investment (P)
  - Satellite backhaul predominant for remote sites in SI (P)
- **Annual maintenance:** 5-7% of capital cost per annum for tower maintenance alone

**UPDATED INFRASTRUCTURE CONTEXT (G, Cl - recent developments 2023-2025):**

**Coral Sea Cable System (Cl most detailed):**
- Operational since January 2020 (Cl) [Note: G mentions "live since 2023" - discrepancy likely refers to different phases]
- Technical specs: 4,700 km submarine cable Honiara to Sydney via PNG, **40 Tbps total capacity**
- Impact: Dramatically reduced international connectivity costs vs. satellite-only
- Solomon Islands Domestic Network: Fiber extended to Auki, Noro, Taro (secondary population centers)

**SINBIP - Solomon Islands National Broadband Infrastructure Project (G, Ge, Cl consensus):**
- **Total deployment:** 161 mobile tower sites across all provinces (Ge confirms "approximately 161 3G/4G sites")
- **Progress (G - most current):** 14 sites operational by September 2024, completion expected by 2026
- **Funding:** Chinese-funded government initiative (Cl)
- **Current partnership:** Solomon Telekom/Our Telekom (Ge notes this creates competitive advantage concern)
- **Strategic opportunity (Ge, Cl):** Tower-sharing arrangements could enable third operator to leverage publicly-funded infrastructure rather than duplicating investment
- **Regulatory concern (Ge):** Public funding inadvertently reinforcing incumbent advantage; third operator should seek regulatory intervention under Part 9 for non-discriminatory access

**BACKHAUL OPTIONS (Cl - most comprehensive):**

| Technology | Latency | Cost/Site/Month | Use Case | Availability |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fiber (Coral Sea Cable) | <10ms | N/A (one-time build) | Urban/secondary cities | Honiara, Auki, Noro, Taro |
| Traditional VSAT | 550-650ms | $500-2,000 | Remote sites | Universally available |
| Starlink LEO Satellite | 20-40ms | $100-500 (est.) | Remote sites (game-changer) | Licensed in SI since August 2024 (Cl) |

**STARLINK STRATEGIC OPPORTUNITY (Cl, G):**
- **SATSOL exclusive reseller:** Satellite Solutions Solomon Islands holds national reseller rights (Cl)
- **Data Garden model (Cl):** SATSOL pioneered combining Starlink backhaul + solar power + community WiFi for remote villages
  - Cost: $15,000-30,000 per site vs. $80,000-150,000 for traditional tower builds
  - Coverage: Remote village connectivity without massive capital deployment
- **Educational deployment (G):** Remote North Vella La Vella schools connected September 2025 - first major satellite rollout for schools

**ENVIRONMENTAL/OPERATIONAL CHALLENGES (Cl - Pacific-specific):**
- **Off-grid power:** Hybrid solar-battery-diesel systems $80,000-150,000 per remote site, plus ongoing fuel logistics
- **Cyclone rating:** Equipment must be Category 5 rated and flood-elevated
- **Salt air corrosion:** Accelerates equipment degradation, reduces lifespan vs. continental deployments
- **Geographic dispersion:** 1,000+ island archipelago, 80% rural population, maritime distances complicate logistics

**ROLLOUT SEQUENCING (P - framework):**
1. **Planning Phase:** Market analysis, business case, spectrum coordination, regulatory approval (3-6 months)
2. **Design Phase:** RF planning, site survey, infrastructure design, backhaul routing (2-4 months)
3. **Build Phase:** Site acquisition, civil works, equipment installation, commissioning (6-12 months for initial urban rollout)
4. **Acceptance Phase:** Network testing, performance validation, live network cutover
5. **Operations Phase:** Ongoing maintenance, capacity management, technology upgrades

**RECOMMENDED PHASING FOR SI THIRD OPERATOR (P, Cl consensus):**
- **Phase 1 (Months 0-6):** Honiara and key provincial centers (Auki, Gizo) with existing infrastructure sites
- **Phase 2 (Months 6-12):** Additional provincial centers and secondary urban areas
- **Phase 3 (Months 12-24):** Rural rollout via satellite/microwave backhaul to achieve 50%+ population coverage
- **Timeline to launch:** 18-24 months from license award to commercial service (P); 30-42 months including regulatory approval (Cl)

**PERFORMANCE OBLIGATIONS (P - Bmobile historical precedent):**
- **Performance bond:** Cash bond typically USD $5-10 million securing network rollout obligations
- **Coverage milestones:** Phased population/geographic coverage targets at intervals (e.g., Year 1: 20%, Year 2: 40%, Year 3: 60%)
- **Annual compliance audits:** Bond drawdown for milestone failures
- **TCSI enforcement precedent (Ge, Cl):** 2010 enforcement of Bmobile coverage obligation identified as primary regulatory challenge - confirms commitments are legally binding

### 4. Distribution and SIM Registration

**MANDATORY SIM REGISTRATION REGIME (P - most detailed regulatory framework):**

**Legal basis:** TCSI directives under Telecommunications Act 2009, Telecommunications (Subscriber Registration) Directives 2023 (C, Ge)

**Requirements for service providers (P primary source):**
- Register all new SIM cards within **24 hours** of activation in provider database
- Upload validated subscriber data to **National Subscriber Registry (NSR)** within **1-5 working days**
- Maintain compliant subscriber information including identity verification:
  - Accepted ID: passport, voter ID, driver's license, birth certificate, NPF membership ID (P)
- Conduct **monthly registration audits** for compliance
- Suspend unregistered or non-compliant SIM cards per regulatory directives

**Authorized agent/dealer network requirements (P):**
- Written authorization of agents, dealers, distributors by service provider
- Documented business licensing (registered business name or limited company registration)
- Verified agent business locations (Honiara area, provincial area, or village designation)
- Valid contact information and employee consent forms for SIM card personnel
- **Monthly compliance inspections** to verify SIM registration procedures
- **Quarterly reports to TCSI** on agent network compliance

**DISTRIBUTION CHANNEL STRATEGY (P framework, Cl competitive analysis):**

**1. Primary channels (high-volume):**
- Operator-owned retail stores in Honiara, provincial capitals (Auki, Gizo, Noro, Munda)
- Major shopping centers and transport hubs

**2. Secondary channels (coverage extension):**
- Authorized mobile phone retailers and electronics shops
- Petrol stations and convenience stores
- Community post offices and trading stores in secondary towns

**3. Digital channels:**
- Online SIM activation (where feasible via digital identity verification)
- Self-service kiosks in urban centers
- **Limitation (P):** Digital penetration limited - 20% internet penetration in SI, high cash-based transaction rates

**DEALER COMPENSATION BENCHMARKS (P - Pacific market standards):**
- Retail margin: 10-15% on SIM card sales
- Activation bonus: USD $0.50-2.00 per new subscriber
- Volume discounts for high-volume dealers
- Training and marketing support for prominent locations

### 5. Customer Acquisition Without Number Portability

**NUMBER PORTABILITY STATUS (P, Cl consensus):**
- **Not currently implemented** in Solomon Islands
- TCSI evaluating implementation frameworks with no announced timeline (P)
- Creates switching friction as primary barrier to customer acquisition

**MARKET CHARACTERISTICS (P - most comprehensive statistics):**
- **Prepaid dominance:** 82% of connections in developing Asia-Pacific markets
- **Churn rates:** 3.1-6.5% per month (37-80% annually) in comparable developing markets
  - Implication: Operators must replace entire customer base every 2-3 years
- **ARPU (Average Revenue Per User):** 2011 data showed 17.1% of monthly income spent on mobile services in SI vs. 7% in Fiji/Samoa (affordability challenge)
- **Customer value focus:** High churn-prone prepaid market requires retention and value-added services for profitability

**DEMOGRAPHIC OPPORTUNITY (Cl):**
- **60% of Solomon Islanders under age 25** - creates strong latent demand for data services

**DUAL-SIM EXPLOITATION STRATEGY (Cl - most strategic):**
- **Dual-SIM phone penetration:** 85% across developing markets (Asia-Pacific leading global adoption)
- **Customer behavior:** Maintain multiple SIMs for:
  - Coverage hedging (different operators cover different areas)
  - Price arbitrage (voice vs. data optimization)
  - Reliability backup
- **Strategic implication:** New entrant competes for "second SIM slot" rather than requiring customers to abandon existing numbers
- **Digicel PNG precedent (Cl):** Within 8 months of entry, SIM prices fell 80% and call rates dropped 60%

**CUSTOMER ACQUISITION TACTICS (P, Cl synthesis):**

**Without number portability, new entrant must compete on:**
1. **Service affordability:** Lower per-minute voice rates and data bundle pricing
2. **Coverage and quality:** Superior signal quality and reliability in populated areas
3. **Value-added services:** Mobile money, international roaming, data packages
4. **Promotional bundling:** Combined voice/SMS/data packages

**Effective acquisition tactics from Pacific markets (Cl):**
- **Free or near-free SIM distribution** at launch
- **On-network calling incentives** creating network effects within social groups
- **Mobile money sign-up bonuses** demonstrating immediate utility
- **Sports and community sponsorship** building brand recognition
- **Enterprise prioritization:** Targeting lower-churn corporate accounts before mass consumer marketing

**RETENTION STRATEGIES (P):**
- **SIM switching friction acknowledgment:** Encourage dual-SIM ownership via promotions
- **Targeted acquisition:** Focus on price-sensitive segments and coverage-deprived areas
- **Loyalty programs:** Automatic top-up discounts, preferential pricing for long-tenure customers
- **Financial identity building:** Link mobile usage to value accumulation (mobile money, credit scoring) to increase switching costs

### 6. Pricing and Market Dynamics

**CURRENT MARKET STRUCTURE (Cl - most current competitive intelligence):**

**Our Telekom (Solomon Telekom Company Ltd):**
- Market share: ~65% (Cl)
- Coverage: All nine provinces
- Technology: 4G network largely Honiara-centric, 2G/3G in provinces
- Ownership: 97% owned by Solomon Islands National Provident Fund (government backing)
- Mobile money: M-SELEN (launched June 2023, 20,000+ users) - vertically integrated, tied to Our Telekom SIMs
- Strengths: Incumbency advantage, government relationships, comprehensive coverage
- Weaknesses: Technology deployment concentrated in capital

**Bmobile-Vodafone (Solomon Islands):**
- Market share: ~35% (Cl)
- Coverage: Only **4 of 9 provinces** (significant geographic limitation)
- Technology: **NO 4G network** - operates only 2G/3G (critical technology gap)
- Ownership: PNG state-owned parent company
- Investment challenge: Reportedly struggled to invest adequately, persistent sale rumors (Cl)
- Innovation: November 2023 partnership with Lynk Global for satellite-to-phone SMS (G, Cl)
- Strengths: Challenger pricing positioning
- Weaknesses: Limited geographic reach, technology lag, investment constraints

**Third operator (SATSOL) - LIMITED ROLE (C):**
- **SATSOL Limited:** Operates broadband services, negotiated interconnection agreements with mobile networks
- **Not a full MNO:** Primarily ISP and infrastructure provider (Cl clarifies role as connectivity partner, not competitor)

**CUSTOMER PAIN POINTS (Cl - competitive opportunities):**
- Rural coverage gaps ("can't get phone reception past Visale")
- Service unreliability in provinces
- High data costs relative to income levels
- Limited 4G availability outside Honiara
- Young demographic (60% under 25) with unmet data service demand

**COMPETITIVE PRICING BENCHMARKS (P - Pacific comparables):**
- **Voice services:** VUV 1.64-2.01 per minute (postpaid vs. prepaid) in Pacific SIDS
- **Data pricing:** USD $0.50-2.00 per MB in developing markets
- **Bundle pricing:** 1GB monthly bundles USD $5-15 typical
- **Trend:** Bundled packages increasingly common, replacing per-unit pricing

**DIFFERENTIATION STRATEGY (Cl - strategic framework):**
- **Primary opportunity:** Rapid 4G/5G deployment across provincial capitals where Bmobile offers only 2G/3G and Our Telekom's 4G footprint hasn't extended
- **Positioning:** "Technology leader" rather than pure price competitor
- **Historical precedent:** Strategy proved effective for Digicel in markets where it could leapfrog incumbent technology

### 7. Operational Systems (BSS/OSS)

**BUSINESS SUPPORT SYSTEMS (BSS) FUNCTIONS (P - comprehensive list):**
- Customer management and ordering systems
- Billing and invoicing (prepaid top-ups, postpaid invoicing)
- Revenue assurance and financial reporting
- Customer care and service delivery tracking
- Provisioning and activation workflows
- Rating and charging engines for calls/SMS/data

**OPERATIONS SUPPORT SYSTEMS (OSS) FUNCTIONS (P):**
- Network configuration and management
- Resource inventory management (spectrum, sites, equipment)
- Fault management and incident response
- Service provisioning automation
- Service assurance and quality monitoring
- SIM provisioning and remote lifecycle management
- Network performance analytics

**INTEGRATION REQUIREMENTS (P - modern standards):**
- **Cloud-native architecture:** Scalability for growing subscriber base, modular design enabling component updates
- **Open APIs:** TM Forum Open Digital Architecture (ODA) standards enabling third-party integration for payment systems, financial services, partner platforms
- **Real-time integration:** Batch daily data consolidation between central database and service provider systems with push/pull data aggregation
- **SIM management automation:** Bulk SIM activation, remote provisioning for eSIMs, automated device suspension for non-payment
- **Interconnection systems:** Automated call routing, rating, and settlement with other operators
- **Regulatory reporting:** Automated subscriber data export to National Subscriber Registry (NSR), frequency usage reporting, compliance monitoring

**VENDOR OPTIONS (P):**
- Established telecom software vendors: Openet (AMDOCS), Comtech, Netcracker, CSG Systems
- Cloud-based solutions: Openminded (Amdocs cloud), Mavenir's cloud RAN management
- Regional vendors with developing market experience

**IMPLEMENTATION TIMELINE (P):**
- **Duration:** 6-12 months from vendor selection to live deployment
- **Resource requirements:** Dedicated IT team and partner systems integrators

**DIGICEL PACIFIC EXPANSION CASE STUDIES (Cl - most detailed historical analysis):**

**PNG Success (2006-2011):**
- **Market entry conditions:** 2% mobile penetration, 100,000 subscribers, K250 ($80) SIM cards
- **Within 8 months:** SIM prices K250 → K5-10 (80% drop), call rates down 60%
- **5-year outcome:** 2.4M subscribers (2,400% increase), 34% mobile penetration
- **Market share achieved:** 91%
- **Economic impact:** World Bank credited liberalization with contributing nearly 1% to PNG's GDP
