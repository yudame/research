# Research Prompt: Episode 4 - Mobile Money from Day One

## Episode Context

**Series:** Solomon Islands Telecom Launch Series (Episode 4 of 6)

**Episode Title:** Mobile Money from Day One - The IumiCash Integration

**Previous Episode Context:**
- Episode 1: Financial ecosystem—80% cash, M-SELEN reached 350k users in 2 years, proving massive mobile money demand
- Episode 2: Duopoly competitive landscape, regulatory framework, strategic positioning for third-player entry
- Episode 3: SATSOL infrastructure partnership enables capital-efficient network deployment via fiber + Starlink

**This Episode Focus:** How partnering with IumiCash (CBSI-licensed mobile money provider with international remittance capability) enables launching with integrated financial services from day one—a capability that took incumbents years to build and creates immediate competitive differentiation.

**Target Audience:** Fintech executives, telecommunications strategists, financial inclusion practitioners, regulatory policy makers, and investors interested in telecom-fintech convergence.

---

## Research Objectives

Understand the competitive dynamics of mobile money in Solomon Islands, the regulatory framework for telecom-affiliated financial services, and how launching with integrated mobile money creates strategic advantages. Analyze IumiCash's unique capabilities (MTS license for remittances, smartphone-based platform) and how they complement or compete with incumbent solutions (M-SELEN's USSD model). Evaluate business model options for telecom + fintech integration.

---

## Core Research Questions

### Mobile Money Landscape

1. **How did M-SELEN (Our Telekom's mobile money) achieve 350,000 users in two years, and what does this reveal about demand?**
   - What customer pain points drove adoption (banking access, remittances, bill payments, P2P transfers)?
   - What features/services proved most popular (what do people actually use mobile money for)?
   - What acquisition channels worked (agent recruitment, marketing, bundling with airtime)?
   - What mistakes or friction points slowed adoption initially?
   - How does M-SELEN usage differ between urban and rural customers?

2. **What is the competitive landscape for mobile money and digital payments?**
   - **M-SELEN:** Market share, agent network size (3,000+ agents), feature set, USSD vs. app usage
   - **EziPei:** Solomon Post-operated, SIM-skin technology, agent footprint (~50 agents), utility payment focus
   - **IumiCash:** Smartphone-only QR payments, merchant network, recent MTS license (April 2025)
   - **Bank mobile banking:** BSP, BRED Bank app-based services for existing account holders
   - What active user numbers exist across platforms (not just registered accounts)?
   - Where do these services overlap vs. serve different customer segments?

3. **What customer use cases drive mobile money adoption?**
   - **P2P transfers:** Sending money to family in other islands/provinces
   - **Utility payments:** Solomon Power prepaid electricity (Cashpower), water bills
   - **Merchant payments:** QR code payments at shops, market vendors
   - **Airtime top-ups:** Self-service mobile credit purchases
   - **Remittances:** Receiving money from diaspora (Australia, NZ, seasonal workers)
   - **Government payments:** Social transfers, salaries
   - Which use cases have highest transaction volume vs. value?

### Competitive Advantages of Integrated Mobile Money

4. **What competitive advantages does launching with integrated mobile money create vs. adding it post-launch?**
   - Customer acquisition: Does bundled telecom + mobile money reduce acquisition costs or increase conversion?
   - Customer retention: Are bundled service customers stickier (lower churn) than telecom-only?
   - Revenue diversification: What revenue models exist (transaction fees, merchant fees, float income, data monetization)?
   - Network effects: How does agent network density affect value proposition?
   - Regulatory advantages: Does launching integrated avoid later licensing/integration challenges?

5. **How does IumiCash's Money Transfer Service (MTS) license enable capabilities that M-SELEN and EziPei lack?**
   - What does the MTS license permit (international remittances, cross-border transactions, currency exchange)?
   - What remittance corridors matter most (Australia → Solomon Islands, NZ → Solomon Islands, seasonal worker programs)?
   - How do remittance fees/speed compare to incumbents (Western Union, KlickEx, Ria, bank transfers)?
   - What customer segments value international remittance capability most?
   - What competitive moat does MTS licensing create (how hard is it for others to get this license)?

### Regulatory Framework

6. **What regulatory requirements exist for telecom-affiliated mobile money under CBSI framework?**
   - What licenses are required to offer mobile money (e-money license, MTS license, payment service provider)?
   - What capital requirements, consumer protection rules, AML/KYC standards apply?
   - Can telecom companies own mobile money operations directly, or must they be separate legal entities?
   - What role did CBSI's Regulatory Sandbox play in IumiCash's development?
   - How does CBSI balance financial inclusion goals with consumer protection and financial stability?

7. **How do you structure the business relationship between telecom operations and payment services?**
   - **Legal structure:** Separate entities vs. divisions, ownership arrangements, licensing implications
   - **Revenue sharing:** How are transaction fees, merchant fees, and other revenues split?
   - **Customer data:** Who owns customer relationships, data sharing agreements, privacy considerations
   - **Agent networks:** Shared distribution vs. separate agent networks, commission structures
   - **Brand positioning:** Co-branding vs. distinct brands for telecom + fintech services
   - Examples from other markets: M-Pesa (Safaricom), GCash (Globe Telecom), JazzCash (Jazz Pakistan)

### Business Model and Pricing

8. **How do you design pricing for bundled telecom + financial services?**
   - What pricing models work: bundled packages, cross-subsidies, freemium, transaction fees?
   - How do you compete on price against M-SELEN (Our Telekom's vertical integration advantage)?
   - What customer segments are most price-sensitive vs. feature-sensitive?
   - How do you price international remittances competitively against Western Union/banks while remaining profitable?

9. **What agent network infrastructure is required, and how does it compare to M-SELEN's 3,000+ agents?**
   - What agent recruitment strategy works (existing retailers, dedicated agents, mobile agent models)?
   - What commission structures attract and retain agents?
   - What training, liquidity management, and support do agents need?
   - How quickly can agent network scale, and what are the costs?
   - Can agent networks be shared between IumiCash and the new telecom operator (SIM sales + cash-in/cash-out)?

### Competitive Positioning

10. **How do you compete against Our Telekom's vertical integration advantage (they own both network and M-SELEN)?**
   - What bundling/promotional advantages does Our Telekom have?
   - How can a partnership model (separate telecom + IumiCash) compete against vertical integration?
   - What customer value propositions differentiate beyond "another mobile money option"?
   - Where is M-SELEN weak or underperforming (rural coverage, remittances, merchant acceptance)?

11. **What role does smartphone penetration play in mobile money strategy?**
   - Only 25% of Solomon Islands has 4G coverage, 20% has 3G, 50% relies on 2G/GSM
   - IumiCash is smartphone-only (QR codes, app-based) while M-SELEN uses USSD (*673#, works on any phone)
   - What are the trade-offs between smartphone-required (better UX, more features) vs. USSD (universal access)?
   - How does device strategy (Episode 6 topic) interact with mobile money adoption?
   - What customer segments can you realistically serve with smartphone-only mobile money?

## Key Considerations

### IumiCash Differentiation
- **MTS license:** International remittance capability (competitive moat)
- **Regulatory Sandbox graduate:** Proven CBSI relationship and compliance track record
- **Smartphone-focused:** Better UX but limits addressable market to smartphone users
- **Smaller agent network:** Less cash-in/cash-out access than M-SELEN's 3,000+ agents

### Market Reality
- M-SELEN has first-mover advantage, massive agent network, and vertical integration with dominant telecom
- 80% cash economy means agent network density is CRITICAL for mobile money success
- Remittance flows are significant economic driver (diaspora in Australia/NZ, seasonal workers)
- Rural customers may prefer USSD (works on any phone) over smartphone apps

### Strategic Questions
- Can a partnership model (telecom + IumiCash) compete with Our Telekom's vertical integration?
- Does MTS license for remittances create sufficient differentiation?
- How quickly can agent network scale to compete with M-SELEN?
- Should mobile money target urban smartphone users first or attempt USSD to reach rural customers?
